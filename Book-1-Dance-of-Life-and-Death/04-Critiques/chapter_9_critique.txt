## 2. Narrative Critique

### Plot & Pacing
**Strengths:** The memorial service (Line 48) provides necessary downtime to process Aric's death. The refugee crisis (Line 346) introduces a new type of conflict (logistics/morality vs. purely military).
**Areas for Improvement:** The Council scene regarding refugees (Line 350) feels a bit straw-man. The opposition ("We barely have supplies") folds very quickly to a moral argument ("It's right"). In reality, logistics don't yield to morality.
**Specific Suggestions:** Make the decision harder. Maybe they have to cut rations immediately, causing unrest, or turn away *some* to save *most*. Kaelen saying "I don't know" (Line 368) is honest, but the solution ("everyone in") should have immediate, visible consequences.

### Character Development
**Strengths:** Drakon and Lyra's conversation (Line 228) is excellent. It deepens their friendship and gives Lyra's unrequited love a respectful outlet. Drakon is surprisingly wise here.
**Areas for Improvement:**
    1.  **Drakon's "Counter-Offer":** (Line 316) yelling over the parapet ("WE MAYBE DO NOT KILL YOU VERY PAINFULLY") borders on parody villain/hero interactions. It undercuts the menace of the Demon King's messenger.
    2.  **Kaelen/Elfiria:** [FIXED] The love confession (Line 178) is very direct. "I'm falling in love with you." It fits the "we might die tomorrow" urgency, but perhaps show them struggling with it more before saying it.
**Specific Suggestions:**
    *   Tone down the "Counter-offer." Maybe Drakon just throws a spear or laughs.
    *   Let the love confession be physically intimate first (the kiss) and verbally confirmed later, or vice versa, to let it breathe.
