# Chapter 12: The Judas Kiss

## Part 1: The Black Dawn

The sentries on the North Wall of Deephollow didn't sound the horn immediately. They were confused.

The mist of the Dead Zone had... solidified. A rolling wall of unnatural darkness was consuming the landscape, eating the light of the rising sun.

"Fog?" a rookie asked.

"Fog doesn't march," the veteran sergeant whispered, gripping the rail. "Sound the alarm."

The horns blew. Low, mournful notes that echoed through the canyon.

Kaelen was already awake. He was in the war room, staring at the map where Finriel's tracker signal had vanished days ago.

"Report!" he barked as Lyra burst in.

"It's Her," Lyra said. Her face was ashen. "She's back."

Kaelen grabbed his helmet. "Is she alone?"

"No," Lyra said. "She brought hell with her."

They ran to the battlements.

Kaelen looked out. His blood ran cold.

Below, thousands of Shadow-Knights stood in perfect silence. They wore armor made of smoke and held blades of obsidian. In the center, riding a nightmare steed, was Finriel.

She was unrecognizable. Her midnight-blue dress swirled like liquid shadow. The crown of thorns on her head pulsed with violet light. *Dawnbringer*, strapped to her back, was a black void in the air.

Beside her rode Seccubus, looking like a lord returning to his estate.

"Finriel!" Kaelen shouted, his voice amplified by magic. "Stand down!"

Finriel looked up. Her eyes were solid violet.

"I am not here to fight, Kaelen," her voice echoed, calm and terrifying. "I am here for the bones."

"Over my dead body," Kaelen growled.

"If you insist," Finriel said.

She raised her hand.

"Sleep."

She didn't cast a spell. She pushed her will onto the city.

A wave of violet energy washed over the walls.

The soldiers on the ramparts slumped over. Hundreds of them. They fell like puppets with cut strings.

"Magic dampeners!" Kaelen roared, activating the ancient runes carved into the wall. "Shields up!"

A golden barrier flickered to life, blocking the sleep-wave. But barely.

"You shield yourself," Finriel noted. "But you trap them inside."

She turned to Seccubus. "Breach the gate."

Seccubus smiled. "With pleasure."

He gestured. The Shadow-Knights surged forward. They didn't run. They flowed like oil. They hit the gate and began to eat it. The iron hissed and dissolved.

"Hold the courtyard!" Kaelen ordered. "Lyra, get the civilians to the deep caves! Drakon, on me!"

"I'm with you, brother," Drakon said, hefting his hammer.

They ran down to the courtyard just as the gates dissolved.

***

## Part 2: The Reunion

Finriel rode into the city she had saved weeks ago.

It was silent. The Sleep spell had worked on the civilians near the gate. Bodies lay everywhere—peaceful, breathing, but unmoving.

"Efficient," Seccubus praised.

"Stay here," Finriel ordered the army. "I go to the Crag alone."

"As you wish, my Queen," Seccubus bowed.

Finriel dismounted. She walked through the silent streets. She passed the bakery. The smithy. The barracks.

She felt no guilt. She was doing this for them. When Elfiria returned, they would understand.

She reached the path to the Crag.

Kaelen stood there.

He was alone. Drakon was... where was Drakon?

"Don't do this, Fin," Kaelen said. He held *World-Breaker* in a low guard. He looked older than ever.

"I'm saving her," Finriel said. "I found a way, Kaelen. Seccubus showed me. We can bring her back."

"It's a lie," Kaelen said, stepping forward. "The dead stay dead. Whatever comes back... it won't be Elfiria. It will be a monster wearing her face."

"Like me?" Finriel asked.

"You are not a monster yet," Kaelen said. "There is still time."

"Time for what?" Finriel scoffed. "Time to be locked up? Time to be erased?"

She drew the corrupted *Dawnbringer*.

"Move, Kaelen."

"No."

Finriel attacked.

She didn't use sword forms. She used telekinesis. She threw *Dawnbringer* at him, guiding it with her mind while she cast spells with her hands.

Kaelen parried the flying sword, sparks flying. *Clang. Clang.*

"Lightning!" Finriel shouted.

Violet lightning arced from her fingers. Kaelen took it on his armor, gritting his teeth as the runes flared hot.

He charged. He was fast. Incredibly fast for a human. He closed the distance, swinging *World-Breaker* in a decapitating arc.

Finriel didn't dodge. She caught the blade.

With her bare hand.

Shadow wrapped around her fingers, stopping the massive sword dead. The ground beneath her cracked from the force, but she didn't budge.

Kaelen's eye went wide.

"I am stronger now," Finriel whispered.

She twisted the sword. *World-Breaker*—a blade forged by dwarves—snapped. The tip fell to the ground.

Kaelen stumbled back, looking at his broken sword. The symbol of his legend. Ruined.

Finriel blasted him.

A wave of force hit him in the chest. Kaelen flew backward, slamming into the rock wall. He slid down, gasping, blood leaking from his armor.

Finriel walked up to him. She summoned *Dawnbringer* back to her hand.

"I told you," she said. "I'm not playing anymore."

She stepped over him and walked up the path to the grave.

## Part 3: The Desecration

The grave was peaceful. The moon lilies were blooming.

Finriel stood before the white stone.

"Hi, Elfiria," she whispered. "I'm here to wake you up."

She raised her hand. *Earth, yield.*

The ground split. The soil moved aside gently, revealing a simple stone coffin.

Finriel lifted the lid with her mind.

Inside lay bones. Just white bones wrapped in decaying white silk. A silver amulet—the symbol of the Moon—rested on the ribcage.

Finriel felt a pang of doubt. It seemed so... final.

*Do it,* Seccubus whispered in her mind. *She is waiting.*

Finriel reached out. She levitated the bones, wrapping them in a cocoon of protective silence.

"Fin!"

Finriel turned.

Thalia came running up the path. She was out of breath, soot-stained, holding her frying pan.

She stopped when she saw the floating bones. Her mouth fell open.

"Fin... what are you doing?"

"I'm taking her," Finriel said. "We're going to bring her back, Thal. Seccubus knows a ritual."

Thalia looked at Kaelen, who was crawling up the path, bleeding. Then she looked at the bones. Then at Finriel.

"That's necromancy," Thalia whispered. "That's evil, Fin."

"It's hope!" Finriel shouted. "If she comes back, the war ends! Kaelen will be happy! We can all go home!"

"Fin, look at you," Thalia stepped closer, ignoring the violet aura. "Your skin... it's grey. Your eyes... you look like *him*."

"I am evolving," Finriel said, reciting the line.

"You're disappearing," Thalia corrected. She reached out a hand. "Please. Put her back. Come with me. We'll run away. Just us. Use the carriage. We'll go to the coast."

"I can't," Finriel said, lowering her head. "I've gone too far. I ate a soul, Thalia. I killed people."

"We can fix it," Thalia cried. "We can fix anything!"

"No," Finriel looked up. "We can't fix the past. We have to rewrite it."

She gestured to the bones. "This is the only way."

She looked at Thalia.

"Come with me," Finriel offered. "Help me. I need you. You're my anchor, remember?"

Thalia looked at Kaelen. Kaelen shook his head weakly. "Don't... go..."

Thalia looked at Finriel. At the pain in her eyes. The desperation.

Thalia dropped the frying pan.

"If I come," Thalia said steadily. "Will you promise not to hurt anyone else today?"

"I promise," Finriel said immediately.

"Then I'm coming," Thalia said.

"Thalia, NO!" Kaelen rasped, trying to stand.

Thalia knelt beside him. She kissed his forehead.

"I have to," she whispered to Kaelen. "She's alone. She needs someone to pull her back."

She stood up and walked to Finriel.

"Let's go," Thalia said.

Finriel smiled. It was a genuine smile, breaking through the corruption for a second.

"Thank you," Finriel said.

She grabbed Thalia's hand. She grabbed the floating bones.

She turned to Seccubus, who was waiting at the bottom of the hill.

"We have them," Finriel called.

Seccubus smiled. He watched Thalia walking down the hill. He licked his lips.

"Perfect," he whispered.

***

## Part 4: The Departure

They left Deephollow in silence.

The Shadow Army withdrew. They didn't kill anyone on the way out. Finriel kept her promise.

Kaelen lay on the path, watching them go. He was broken. Physically and spiritually.

Drakon found him an hour later.

"Kaelen!" Drakon knelt, checking the wounds. "You're alive. Tough old bastard."

"She took them," Kaelen whispered. "She took Elfiria."

"We'll get her back," Drakon promised, helping him up.

"And Thalia," Kaelen said, clutching Drakon's arm. "She took Thalia."

Drakon went still.

"Why?"

"I don't know," Kaelen said, staring at the empty grave. "But whatever Seccubus is planning... Thalia is part of it."

He looked at his broken sword.

"I need a new weapon," Kaelen growled. The pain in his voice was gone, replaced by cold, hard hatred.

"We're going to the Spire," Kaelen said. "And we are going to kill them all."

***

## Part 5: The Catalyst

In the Spire of Whispers, Seccubus led Finriel and Thalia to the ritual chamber.

He placed Elfiria's bones on a black altar.

"Is this it?" Finriel asked. "Are we ready?"

"Almost," Seccubus said. He looked at Thalia.

Thalia was looking around the room, shivering. The air smelled of ozone and death.

"You are Thalia," Seccubus said smoothly. "The faithful servant."

"I'm her friend," Thalia corrected, stepping closer to Finriel.

"Of course," Seccubus bowed. "Friendship is a powerful thing. Rare. Pure."

He walked to a second altar—a smaller one, etched with silver runes.

"Finriel," Seccubus said. "The ritual requires a Catalyst. An anchor to the living world. Someone to hold the soul of the departed while it transitions."

"I'll do it," Finriel said.

"No," Seccubus shook his head. "You are the Caster. You must weave the spell. You cannot be the anchor."

He pointed to Thalia.

"It must be her."

Finriel froze. "Thalia? Is it dangerous?"

"Not at all," Seccubus lied effortlessly. "She just needs to lie there and think of Elfiria. Or think of you. Her love for you will act as a lighthouse."

Finriel looked at Thalia. "Thal... you don't have to."

Thalia looked at the bones. She looked at Finriel's desperate face.

"Will it save you?" Thalia asked Finriel intently. "If we do this... will you stop? Will you be happy?"

"Yes," Finriel nodded, tears leaking violet. "I will be whole again."

"Then I'll do it," Thalia said.

She walked to the small altar and lay down.

"It's cold," she whispered.

"I'll make it quick," Finriel promised. "Just a nap."

Seccubus stood in the shadows, his eyes gleaming.

"Just a nap," he echoed.

Finriel began to chant.

And as the violet light filled the room, she didn't see the rune Seccubus traced on Thalia's forehead.

The rune for *Sacrifice*.
