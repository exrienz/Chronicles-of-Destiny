# Chapter 2: The Crossing

## Part 1: The Severing

The sunrise was wrong. That was the first thing Finriel noticed as the carriage rumbled through the massive, rune-etched gates of Silverglade’s southern perimeter. Inside the Holy Dome, the sun was always a perfect, warm gold—a gentle embrace that promised growth and safety. But as they passed the threshold, the light changed.

It became thinner. Sharper.

Finriel leaned out the window, watching the shimmering, prismatic wall of the Dome recede behind them. It rose like a cliff of crystallized light, disappearing into the clouds miles above. It was beautiful. It was terrifying. It was the only world she had ever known.

"Don't look back," Thalia’s voice was small, trembling. She was sitting on the opposite bench, knees pulled up to her chest, clutching a bag of dried apples as if it were a talisman against evil. "It’s bad luck, Fin. My nan said looking back at the safety you’re leaving invites the darkness to follow you."

Finriel pulled her head back inside, smoothing the silk of her travel dress. Even her clothes felt out of place here. The carriage interior was upholstered in velvet, smelling of lavender and old wood, a tiny bubble of Sylvanarian luxury moving through a dying world.

"We have to look back to remember what we're fighting for," Finriel said, reciting a line from the *Codex of Duty*. It tasted like ash in her mouth. She wished, just for a moment, that she wasn't the Chosen One. That she was just a girl going on a trip.

"I'm fighting for not getting eaten," Thalia muttered, taking a fierce bite of an apple. "And for you. Mostly for you."

The carriage jolted violently, throwing them both against the cushions.

"Apologies, My Lady!" the driver called out, his voice thin with nerves. " The road... the pavement ends here!"

Finriel looked out again. The smooth, white-stone road of the Kingdom had vanished. In its place was a track of cracked earth, dry and grey, wind-scoured into ruts that looked like scars.

They were out.

Beyond the Dome’s influence, the world was... grey. The grass was a dull, desaturated brown. The trees were twisted, their bark black, their leaves missing or hanging in tattered strips. The sky was an oppressively low ceiling of churning clouds, the color of a bruised plum.

And the silence.

Inside the Dome, there was always sound—birds, waterfalls, the hum of mana. Here, there was only the wind. A low, moaning wind that sounded like it was in pain.

"Is this..." Finriel whispered, her hand going to the hilt of *Dawnbringer*. "Is this the Corruption?"

*This is the Void's touch, little bird.*

Aria’s voice was louder here. Uncomfortably distinct. Finriel flinched, looking around, but Thalia was just chewing her apple, staring at the floor.

*The Dome hides the truth,* Aria whispered. *It paints a pretty picture over a rotting canvas. This... this is reality. Do you feel it? The hunger?*

Finriel shivered. She *did* feel it. It was an itch under her skin, a pressure in her headache. The mana in the air here was thin, erratic. Trying to draw on it felt like trying to drink through a straw that had holes in it.

"It's ugly," Finriel said aloud.

"It's dead," Thalia corrected. She shimmied over to sit next to Finriel, seeking warmth. "Everything looks dead."

They rode in silence for hours. The landscape didn't change, only the intensity of the desolation. They passed ruined farmhouses, their roofs caved in, overgrown with strange, purple-veined vines. They passed a village that had been burned to the ground so long ago that only the stone chimneys remained, standing like tombstones.

"Where are the people?" Thalia asked quietly.

"Gone," Finriel said. "Fled to the inner kingdoms. Or..."

"Or eaten," Thalia finished. She shuddered.

Around midday, the carriage slowed.

"What is it?" Finriel called out.

"Check point, My Lady!" the driver yelled back. "Exiled territory border!"

Exiled Territory. The words sent a thrill of nervousness through Finriel. The Exiled were legends inside the Dome—boogeymen and heroes wrapped in one. They were the soldiers who refused to retreat, who lived in the hellscape to hold the line.

The carriage stopped.

Finriel opened the door and stepped out. The air hit her like a physical blow—cold, dry, and smelling of sulfur.

Ahead of them, a barricade blocked the road. It wasn't elegant. It was a pile of rubble, sharpened stakes, and rusted iron plates welded together. Atop the barricade stood three figures.

They didn't look like the Royal Guard. Their armor was mismatched—scavenged plates of steel, leather, and beast-hide. Their cloaks were stained with mud. But their weapons... their weapons were clean.

"Halt!" the center figure barked. He was a human, heavily scarred, holding a crossbow. "Identify!"

"I am Finriel Silverbreeze," she called out, standing tall, trying to project the authority Elara had taught her. "Ambassador of the Alliance. We are expected at Deephollow."

The crossbow didn't lower. "Ambassador, huh? You look like dessert to me."

The soldiers laughed, a harsh, grating sound.

"We have papers!" the driver squeaked, waving the golden scroll.

"Papers don't stop void-beasts," the soldier spat. He slid down the barricade, landing with a heavy thud. He walked up to Finriel, looming over her. He smelled of sweat, old blood, and tobacco.

Finriel didn't flinch. She met his gaze. His eyes were hard, skeptical.

"Pretty sword," he nodded at *Dawnbringer*. "You know how to use it, Princess? or is it a letter opener?"

"It is a blade forged to kill demons," Finriel said coolly. "And I know how to use it."

The soldier snorted. "We'll see. Open the gate! Let the 'Ambassador' through. If she dies on the road, I want her boots."

The barricade was dragged aside. Finriel climbed back into the carriage, her heart hammering.

"They're rude," Thalia hissed. "And mean."

"They're alive," Finriel said softly. "Out here... maybe that's all that matters."

## Part 2: First Blood

Night fell quickly in the Outer Lands. It wasn't a gradual darkening; the sun simply vanished behind the western peaks, and the world plunged into absolute blackness. There were no stars. The cloud cover choked them out.

They had been forced to stop. The horses were exhausted, foaming at the mouth from the bad air. The driver had pulled the carriage into the shelter of a ruined stone tower—an old watchtower from before the war.

"We camp here," the driver said, his voice shaking. "I... I will tend the horses."

Finriel and Thalia set up a small fire inside the base of the tower. The stones blocked the wind, but the cold seeped up through the ground.

"I hate this," Thalia mumbled, wrapped in three blankets. "It's so quiet. It's unnatural."

Finriel sat cross-legged, staring into the weak flames. She was trying to meditate, to replenish her mana reserves, but the flow was sluggish.

*They are watching.*

The voice sliced through her concentration.

Finriel’s eyes snapped open. "What?"

"I said I hate this," Thalia repeated.

"No... not you." Finriel stood up, hand on her sword. "Something is out there."

"Don't say that," Thalia whimpered. "Please don't say that."

"Driver!" Finriel called out.

Silence.

"Driver?"

Finriel stepped out of the tower ruin. The horses were whickering nervously, stamping their hooves. The driver was nowhere to be seen.

"Hello?"

Then she heard it. A wet, tearing sound. Like someone ripping a wet cloth.

It came from behind the carriage.

Finriel drew *Dawnbringer*. The blade glowed with a faint, pale luminescence, casting long, dancing shadows against the rocks.

"Stay inside, Thalia," she commanded. Her voice was steady, but her hands were trembling. This was it. Real. No automatons. No Instructor Elara to scream 'Halt'.

She moved around the carriage, step by cautious step.

The driver was there. He was on the ground. Or... parts of him were.

Crouched over him was a creature. It was the size of a wolf, but hairless, its skin a slick, oily black that seemed to absorb the light from her sword. It had too many limbs. Spines protruded from its back. And its face... it had no face. Just a vertical slit of a mouth filled with needle-teeth.

A Void-Scavenger. Lowest class of demon. But real.

The creature looked up. Blood dripped from its maw. It hissed, a sound like steam escaping a vent.

Finriel froze. The smell hit her first—copper and rot. Then the sight. The driver’s eyes were open, staring at nothing.

She wanted to vomit. She wanted to scream. She wanted to run back to the Dome and never leave.

*Strike, little bird! Before it strikes you!*

Aria screamed the command.

The scavenger lunged. It moved with terrifying speed, a blur of motion.

Finriel didn't think. Instinct took over. Not the instinct of a warrior, but the instinct of prey. She raised her sword in a panic, squeezing her eyes shut, and screamed.

"LIGHT!"

She poured every ounce of panicked mana she had into the blade.

*Flash.*

A blinding explosion of white light erupted from *Dawnbringer*. It wasn't a beam; it was a bomb. The shockwave threw Finriel backward, slamming her into the carriage wheel.

When her vision cleared, blinking away the spots, she looked at the scavenger.

It was... gone.

Where the creature had been, there was a scorch mark on the ground in the shape of a shadow. The rock around it was smoking.

"Fin!" Thalia came running out, clutching a frying pan she had packed. She skidded to a halt, looking at the scorch mark, then at the driver’s remains. She gagged, turning away to retch.

Finriel sat up, gasping for air. Her arm throbbed. She had used too much mana. Way too much. That spell should have illuminated a room; instead, it had vaporized a demon.

"I... I killed it," she whispered.

*Sloppy,* Aria critiqued. *But effective. You wasted 40% of your reserves on a scavenger. If a Hunter comes, you are dead.*

"Shut up," Finriel muttered, clutching her head.

"Fin?" Thalia wiped her mouth, teary-eyed. "Are you okay?"

"I... I think so." Finriel stood up, legs shaking. She looked at the driver. "We... we have to bury him."

"We can't," Thalia sniffled. "We don't have shovels. And... the ground is too hard."

Finriel looked at the vast, dark emptiness around them. The driver was just... gone. Just meat now.

"Then we burn him," Finriel said, her voice sounding hollow, like it belonged to someone else. "We can't leave him for them."

They spent the next hour gathering dry brush. It was gruesome work. Thalia cried the whole time. Finriel didn't cry. She felt numb. Cold.

As the pyre burned, sending sparks up into the oppressive clouds, Finriel stared into the fire.

"This is it," she realized. "This is the war."

It wasn't glorious. It wasn't heroic. It was dirty, smelly, and sad.

*Yes,* Aria whispered. *It is pain. But pain is power, Finriel. Use it.*

Finriel looked at her hands. They were stained with soot and the driver's blood. The hero marks glowed dimly, pulsing in time with the dying fire.

"We have to drive," Finriel said, turning to the horses. "We can't stay here. The light will attract more."

"You know how to drive a carriage?" Thalia asked, voice wobbling.

"No," Finriel climbed onto the driver's seat, taking the reins. They felt heavy, slick with damp night air. "But I'm the Chosen One. I'm supposed to know everything, right?"

She snapped the reins. "Hya!"

The horses, eager to leave the scent of blood, bolted forward. The carriage careened into the darkness, carrying two terrified girls deeper into the nightmare.

## Part 3: The Shadow of the Spire

Two days later, they saw it.

Deephollow.

It wasn't a city so much as a wound in the earth that had been stitched shut with iron and stone. The road wound down into a massive canyon, the walls of which were honeycombed with caves and structures. Towers rose from the gloom, torches flickering like vigilant eyes.

And in the center, rising from the canyon floor, was the Spire of Anguish—no, wait. That was the Demon King's tower. This... this was the Citadel of the Exiled. A blocky, utilitarian fortress that looked like a fist punching the sky.

Finriel guided the exhausted horses down the switchback road. She hadn't slept in thirty hours. Her eyes burned. Her mana was dangerously low. Thalia was asleep inside, exhausted from terror.

As they approached the massive iron gates, Finriel straightened her spine. She tried to wipe the soot from her face, tried to smooth her ruined dress.

*Look strong,* she told herself. *Don't let them see you crumble.*

The gates creaked open before she even hailed them. Soldiers lined the walls—hundreds of them. Silent. Watching.

She drove the carriage into the main courtyard. It was crowded. Men and women in mismatched armor, civilians in rags, children with too-old eyes. They all stared at the white carriage, now splattered with grey mud.

A space cleared in the center.

A man walked forward.

Finriel knew him instantly from the history books. Kaelen. The Sword Saint.

He was bigger than the paintings. He looked like a mountain that learned to walk. His armor was black, scarred. His hair was grey. And that scar... it carved his face in two.

He stopped in front of the horses, grabbing the bridle of the lead stallion. The animal, wild with fear for two days, calmed instantly under his touch.

Finriel climbed down. Her legs felt like jelly. She stumbled, momentarily, but caught herself against the wheel.

She looked up at him.

"I am Finriel Silverbreeze," she croaked. Her throat was raw. "I... I have come."

Kaelen looked at her. He looked at the carriage, at the soot on her face, at the dried blood on her sleeve that wasn't hers. He looked at the scorch mark on the side of the carriage where *Dawnbringer*'s blast had singed the wood.

He didn't bow. He didn't smile.

"Where is your driver?" Kaelen asked.

"Dead," Finriel said. "Scavenger. Two nights ago."

"And you made it here alone?"

"I drove," she said. "We didn't stop."

Kaelen’s single grey eye narrowed. He looked at the carriage door opening, where Thalia was peeking out, clutching her frying pan.

He looked back at Finriel.

"You're alive," he said. It sounded almost like an accusation.

"I am," Finriel said. She stood up straighter, lifting her chin. "I told you. I am ready to fight."

Kaelen snorted. He turned to the crowd of soldiers.

"Well?" he shouted. "Show her!"

The soldiers slammed their spears against their shields. *Crash. Crash. Crash.*

It wasn't a cheer. It was a rhythm. A war beat.

"Welcome to hell, Princess," Kaelen said, turning back to her. "Try to survive the night."

He walked away.

Finriel stood there, the rhythm of the spears vibrating in her bones. She felt a wave of dizziness.

*He mocks you,* Aria hissed. *He thinks you are weak. You encountered a demon and lived. You saved your servant. You traveled the waste alone. You are strong.*

"I am strong," Finriel whispered to herself.

She took Thalia's hand as her friend climbed down.

"We made it," Thalia squeaked.

"We made it," Finriel agreed. She looked up at the looming fortress, at the grey sky that pressed down on them.

She didn't feel like a hero. She felt like fresh meat.

But she was here. And she would not run.

*Good,* Aria purred. *Let the game begin.*

## Part 4: The Night Watch

Finriel lay on the straw mattress in the barracks. It was scratchy and smelled of mildew. The stone walls leaked cold air. All around her, the sounds of sleeping soldiers—snores, coughs, muttered nightmares—filled the room.

Kaelen hadn't put them in guest quarters. He put them here. With the grunts.

Thalia was asleep in the bunk above her, exhausted.

Finriel couldn't sleep. Every time she closed her eyes, she saw the driver's face. She saw the scavenger's teeth.

She rolled over, staring at the stone ceiling.

"Why?" she whispered. "Why me?"

*Because you can take it,* Aria answered.

"I don't think I can."

*You can. You have the fire. I felt it when you burned the scavenger. It was exquisite.*

Finriel frowned. "It was messy. I panicked."

*Panic is energy. Fear is fuel. You just need to learn how to channel it. The old woman in the Dome... Elara... she taught you to be a garden. Neat. Orderly. Safe.*

*But war is a forest fire, Finriel. You must be the fire.*

"I don't want to be fire," Finriel whispered. tears leaking from her eyes. "I just want to go home."

*There is no home anymore,* the voice was hard now. Cold. *There is only the fight. Kaelen knows this. That is why he hates you. He sees the weakness of the Dome in you.*

*Do you want him to be right?*

Finriel wiped her eyes. "No."

*Then sleep. Tomorrow, we show him fire.*

Finriel closed her eyes. The voice... Aria... she was right. Kaelen looked at her like she was a joke. Like the driver’s death was her fault.

She clenched her fist, the hero marks pulsing faintly under the blanket.

*I will show him,* she thought. *I will show them all.*

And for the first time, in the darkness of the barracks, the comforting voice of her imaginary friend didn't feel quite so imaginary. It felt real. It felt... powerful.

And Finriel, alone and terrified, reached out for that power like a drowning girl reaching for a rope.

She didn't see the rope was a noose.
