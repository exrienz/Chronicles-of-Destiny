# Chapter 4: Iron and Glass

## Part 1: The Arena

The Training Pits of Deephollow were not designed for sport. They were holes dug into the rock, filled with sand, gravel, and dried blood. There were no stands, only the sloping sides of the crater where off-duty soldiers stood in silent clusters, watching.

Dawn had broken, painted in shades of bruised purple and charcoal grey. The air was frigid, mist clinging to the ground like spectral fingers.

Finriel stood at one end of the Pit. She had forgone her heavy travel cloak, wearing only her fitted combat tunic and leggings under the polished mythril breastplate. *Dawnbringer* was drawn, the blade glowing with a steady, white light that seemed offensive in the gloom.

Her breath plumed in the air. Her heart was a war drum in her chest.

"He's late," Thalia whispered from the edge of the Pit, hopping from foot to foot to keep warm. She was holding a towel and a water skin, looking like a squire for a knight in a storybook.

"He's making me wait," Finriel said tight-lipped. "It's a tactic. Psychological warfare. Elara taught me about it."

She spun *Dawnbringer* in a flourish, the hum of the blade cutting the silence. She felt good. Sharp. She had meditated all night, channeling the ambient mana of Deephollow—which was dirty and chaotic, but potent—into her core. She was fully charged.

"Make way!"

The soldiers on the ridge parted.

Kaelen descended.

He wasn't wearing his armor.

Finriel blinked. He wore simple canvas trousers and a sleeveless woolen tunic. His arms were bare, revealing a map of scars—burns, cuts, bites—that told the story of a hundred battles. He carried no shield. On his back wasn't the massive greatsword *World-Breaker*, but a simple wooden practice sword, weighted with lead.

He walked into the Pit, his boots crunching on the gravel. He looked bored.

"You're not wearing armor," Finriel accused, lowering her sword slightly.

Kaelen stretched his neck, audibly cracking it to the left, then the right. "Armor is for people who plan on getting hit."

Finriel flushed. "This is an honorable duel! I will not fight an unarmed opponent!"

"There is no honor in a demon pit, girl," Kaelen said, spinning the wooden sword in his hand. It moved with terrifying speed, heavy and blunt. "And I'm not unarmed. I have a stick."

Laughter rippled through the soldiers on the ridge. Finriel heard Grog's distinct snort.

"Fine," Finriel hissed. "If you want to be broken, I will break you."

She adopted the High Guard stance of the Silverglade Duelists—sword high, left hand extended for casting.

Kaelen didn't take a stance. he just stood there, sword hanging loosely at his side, weight centered.

"Begin," Lyra called from the judge's stand.

## Part 2: The Lesson

Finriel didn't hesitate. She launched forward, augmenting her speed with a burst of wind magic. *Accelerate.*

She crossed the twenty paces in a heartbeat, thrusting *Dawnbringer* toward Kaelen's shoulder—a non-lethal spot, but painful.

Kaelen didn't move. He didn't flinch.

Just as the tip of her blade was about to touch his tunic, he shifted. A single step to the left. Just enough. The blade hissed through empty air.

Finriel stumbled but used her momentum to spin into a horizontal slash. "Ignis!" she shouted.

Flame erupted along her blade, a trail of fire meant to catch him even if the steel missed.

Kaelen ducked under the fire, the heat singing his hair. He brought the pommel of his wooden sword up, slamming it into Finriel’s solar plexus.

*THUD.*

It wasn't a hard hit—he checked it—but it knocked the wind out of her. Finriel gasped, staggering back.

"Too flashy," Kaelen critiqued, his voice calm. "You announced your attack with your mana before you even moved. A blind demon could have dodged that."

Finriel wheezed, sucking in air. Anger flared hot in her chest. "Lucky... dodge."

"Luck is a resource," Kaelen said. "I rely on skill."

Finriel roared, slamming her hand into the ground. "Roots of the Earth!"

 Vines—corrupted and thorny from the local soil—erupted beneath Kaelen’s feet.

He jumped. Not a normal jump, but a gravity-defying leap, kicking off the rising vines as stepping stones. He was airborne, coming down on top of her.

Finriel raised her sword to block.

*CLACK.*

The heavy wooden sword hit the flat of *Dawnbringer*. The force was immense. Finriel’s knees buckled. She channeled mana into her arms to hold the block.

*Shatter him!* Aria screamed in her mind. *Use the Blast!*

"Sunburst!" Finriel screamed.

A sphere of blinding light exploded from her chest armor. It was a defensive spell designed to stun anyone in melee range.

Kaelen shut his eye a split second before the flash. He took the concussive force on his shoulder, sliding back through the gravel.

Finriel seized the opening. She charged through the fading light, sword raised for a downward cleave.

"I have you!"

Kaelen opened his eye. He caught her wrist with his left hand—his grip like an iron vise—stopping the blow mid-swing.

"Do you?" he asked.

He twisted her wrist. Finriel shrieked as *Dawnbringer* dropped from her numbed fingers. Kaelen swept her legs with his own, and she hit the dirt hard.

Before she could scramble up, the heavy wooden sword was pressed against her throat.

"Dead," Kaelen said.

The Pit was silent.

Finriel lay there, chest heaving, staring up at the grey sky and the weary face of the Sword Saint.

"You... you cheated," she choked out. "You grabbed me."

Kaelen pulled the sword away and offered her a hand. Finriel slapped it away and scrambled to her feet, ignoring the gravel embedded in her palms.

"This was a sword duel!" she yelled, tears of frustration stinging her eyes. "You used grapple techniques!"

Kaelen sighed. He tossed the wooden sword to the ground. "In the wild, a demon will not fence with you, Finriel. It will bite your face off. It will rip your arm off and beat you with it. There are no rules. There is only alive and dead."

He gestured to the watching soldiers. "Look at them."

Finriel looked up. The soldiers weren't laughing anymore. They were watching with grim expressions.

"They survive because they know that honor is a luxury," Kaelen said. "You rely on your mana. You rely on your forms. You rely on your shiny sword." He kicked *Dawnbringer*, sending it skittering across the dirt. "Take away the magic, and what are you?"

"I am the Chosen One!" Finriel screamed, her mana flaring uncontrollably. The air around her crackled with green lightning. "I was born to save you! I have power you can't even imagine!"

"Power," Kaelen said softly, stepping closer until he was inside her guard. "Is useless if you don't know *why* you fight."

He pointed to the scar on his chest. "I fight because I failed. I fight because the woman I loved died to give us a chance. What do you fight for, Finriel? Because a Council told you to? Because a prophecy said so?"

Finriel opened her mouth, but no words came.

"Go clean up," Kaelen said, turning his back. "Training starts at noon. And bring a real weapon this time. Not a glow-stick."

Finriel stood trembling in the center of the Pit. The humiliation washed over her like cold water. She had lost. In front of everyone. To an old man with a stick.

She grabbed *Dawnbringer* and ran. She ran past Thalia, past Lyra, past the staring soldiers. She didn't stop until she reached the only place where she could be alone.

The edge of the cliff, overlooking the Dead Zone.

## Part 3: The Crack

Finriel sat on the edge of the precipice, legs dangling over the abyss. Below, the Dead Zone stretched out into infinity—a sea of swirling grey mist and black rock.

She was crying. Ugly, hiccuping sobs that shook her armor.

"I hate him," she whispered to the wind. "I hate this place."

*He humiliated you,* the voice said.

Aria wasn't whispering anymore. She sounded... solid. Real.

*He is jealous, little bird. He sees your light and it blinds him. He wants to break you down so he can control you.*

"He's right," Finriel sniffled. "I was slow. I relied on the forms."

*No,* Aria soothed. *You held back. You were afraid of hurting him. If you had used the Void-Root... if you had used the Black Fire... he would be ash.*

"I don't know Black Fire," Finriel said, confused. "Elara never taught me that."

*Elara hid things from you. The Council hid things. Because they fear what you could become.*

The mist below seemed to swirl, rising up towards her feet. It felt cool. Inviting.

*They want you to be a shield, Finriel. A pretty wall to stand between them and the dark. But you are not a shield. You are a sword.*

*Let show you.*

Finriel felt a sensation—like cold fingers touching the back of her neck. It wasn't unpleasant. It was... numbing. It took away the sting of the humiliation. It took away the fear.

*Close your eyes.*

Finriel closed her eyes.

In the darkness, she saw a symbol. A rune she had never seen before. It was jagged, sharp, pulsing with a deep violet light.

*Reach for it,* Aria whispered.

Finriel reached out with her mind. She touched the rune.

**POWER.**

It rushed into her like a flood. Not the warm, sunlight mana of the Dome. This was cold. Dark. Heavy. It tasted like iron and ozone. It felt stronger than anything she had ever felt.

She gasped, her eyes snapping open.

Her hand was glowing. But not with green light. For a fleeting second, faint wisps of violet smoke were curling around her fingers.

She shook her hand, and the smoke vanished.

"What was that?" she whispered, terrified.

*That was potential,* Aria said. *That was the truth. Keep it safe, little bird. Keep it hidden. When Kaelen tries to break you again... and he will... remember the power.*

Finriel stared at her hand. She should be scared. She should go to Lyra. She should tell someone.

But the humiliation still burned in her chest. The memory of the soldiers laughing. Kaelen's dismissive eyes.

This power... it didn't judge her. It just *was*.

"Fin?"

Thalia’s voice broke the trance. Finriel jumped, scrambling back from the edge.

Thalia stood a few feet away, holding a fresh tunic. She looked worried.

"Are you okay? You've been out here for hours."

"I'm fine," Finriel said quickly, hiding her hands behind her back. "Just... thinking."

"Kaelen is a jerk," Thalia said firmly, sitting down beside her. "He didn't have to be so mean. You did great. That light blast? It was amazing!"

"It missed," Finriel said dully.

"He cheated!" Thalia insisted. "But... maybe he has a point? About the survival thing?"

Finriel looked at her friend. Thalia was so innocent. So good. She deserved a hero who could protect her. Not a girl who got beaten with a stick.

"He has a point," Finriel admitted. She stood up, dusting off her armor. "But I'll learn. I'll learn his way. And then I'll beat him."

She looked back at the Dead Zone one last time. The mist seemed to wave at her.

*Soon,* Aria whispered.

"Come on," Finriel said. "Let's go to training. I don't want to be late."

## Part 4: The Grinder

The next week was a blur of pain.

Kaelen didn't teach. He dismantled.

Every morning, from dawn until noon, Finriel was in the Pit. She fought Kaelen. She fought Drakon. She fought Grog. She fought three soldiers at once.

She lost. A lot.

She learned what mud tasted like. She learned how to splint a broken finger. She learned that a fireball doesn't matter if someone throws sand in your eyes while you're casting.

But she also learned.

She learned to cast without speaking. She learned to use her staff to hook legs. She learned to use the environment—kicking gravel, collapsing sand piles.

And she began to change.

The pristine armor was scratched and dented. She stopped polishing it. The silk dresses were gone, replaced by simple wool tunics that smelled of sweat and woodsmoke. Her hair, once elaborate, was chopped short with a knife to keep it out of her eyes.

She looked less like a doll. She looked like a soldier.

But inside, something else was changing too.

Every time she failed, every time she felt weak, the Voice was there. Aria became her constant companion. When Kaelen yelled, Aria soothed. When the soldiers laughed, Aria promised revenge.

Finriel started using the 'cold mana' more often. Just in small bursts. To sharpen her blade. To harden her skin. No one noticed. The violet tint was too faint to see in the battle chaos.

But Lyra noticed.

Lyra watched from the ridge, day after day. She saw the way Finriel moved—sharper, more aggressive. She saw the shadows under the girl's eyes deepen.

"She's improving fast," Drakon commented, watching Finriel flip over Grog and kick him in the kidney.

"Too fast," Lyra murmured. "She's not sleeping, Drakon. And her mana signature... it feels... heavy."

"She's adapting to the void-taint," Drakon shrugged. "We all do. My scales turned grey in a year. It changes you."

"Maybe," Lyra said, rubbing her temple. "Or maybe something else is changing her."

Below in the Pit, Finriel disarmed Grog, holding her blade to his throat.

"Yield," she hissed. Her eyes were wide, the pupils dilated.

"I yield, Princess!" Grog laughed, tapping out.

Finriel didn't lower the sword immediately. For a second, her hand trembled.

*Do it,* Aria whispered. *One thrust. Silence the laughter forever.*

Finriel blinked, the red haze fading. She pulled the sword back.

"Good fight," she muttered, helping Grog up.

"You're getting meaner," Grog grinned, slapping her on the back. "I like it."

Finriel forced a smile. But as she walked away, her hand was shaking. Not from exertion. From fear.

Because for a second... just a second... she had *wanted* to do it.

She looked up at the ridge. Kaelen was watching her. He wasn't smiling. He looked... sad.

"What are you seeing, old man?" she thought.

*He sees his replacement,* Aria said. *And he is terrified.*

Finriel wiped the sweat from her forehead. A dark smear remained, like war paint.

"Let him be terrified," she whispered.
