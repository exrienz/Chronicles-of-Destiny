# Chapter 7: Whispers and Screams

## Part 1: The Cold Carriage

Finriel was back in the wasteland.

The sky was the color of a bruise, purple and black, swirling with storm clouds that looked like faces. She was sitting in the driver's seat of the white carriage, her hands gripping the reins.

The horses were skeletons. Bleached white bones held together by black sludge, their hooves striking sparks on the stone road.

Beside her sat the Driver.

He was dead. But not the clean death of the pyre. He was a ruin. His jaw was hanging by a strip of skin. His eyes were empty sockets that wept black oil.

"Lovely night for a drive, My Lady," the Driver croaked. His voice was wet. Bubbling.

"You're dead," Finriel whispered, staring straight ahead. "I burned you."

"Did you?" The Driver turned his head with a sickening crunch of vertebrae. "Or did you just burn the evidence? You were so scared, little bird. So sloppy."

"I tried to save you," Finriel said, her hands shaking on the reins.

"You saved yourself," the Driver laughed. Black beetles scuttled out of his mouth. "You flashed the light. You drew them to me. You made me the bait."

"No!" Finriel screamed. "That's not true!"

"Isn't it?"

The voice changed. It wasn't the Driver anymore. It was deeper. Smoother. A voice like velvet wrapped around a razor blade.

Finriel looked. Ideally, the Driver should have been there. Instead, there was a shadow. A man made of smoke, wearing a suit of impeccable, dark noble clothes. He had no face, only a smooth white mask with a painted smile.

Seccubus.

"You have always been selfish, Finriel," the Shadow said, leaning in. "Even in the garden. You wanted to be the hero. Heroes need victims."

"I am the Chosen One," Finriel sobbed.

"You are a weapon," Seccubus corrected. He reached out a gloved hand and touched her face. His touch was freezing. "And weapons don't have friends. They have targets."

He pointed ahead.

Thalia was standing in the road.

She was holding her frying pan, looking small and terrified.

"Fin?" Thalia called out. "Stop! Please stop!"

Finriel pulled on the reins. "Whoa! Stop!"

The skeletal horses didn't stop. They accelerated. They screamed, a sound like tearing metal.

"I can't stop!" Finriel yelled at Thalia. "Move! THALIA, MOVE!"

Thalia didn't move. She just stared, tears streaming down her face.

"You promised," Thalia whispered.

The carriage hit her.

There was no sound of impact. Just a burst of violet light. Thalia exploded into a cloud of ash.

Finriel screamed.

## Part 2: The Assessment

"WAKE UP!"

Finriel gasped, bolting upright.

She wasn't in the carriage. She was in her bunk.

But something was wrong.

The room was freezing. Her breath plumed in the air. Frost covered the stone walls in intricate, fern-like patterns. And the bed... her bed was alive.

Thick, black vines had erupted from the mattress, wrapping around her legs, her arms, her chest. They were pulsing, drinking her heat.

"Get it off!" she shrieked, thrashing.

She couldn't move. The vines were like iron cables.

"Finriel! Calm down!"

The door burst open. Kaelen charged in, *World-Breaker* drawn.

He saw her trapped in the vines. He saw the frost. He saw the violet light leaking from her eyes.

"Don't kill it!" Finriel screamed, terrified he would strike her.

Kaelen sheathed his sword instantly. He grabbed a torch from the wall sconce and thrust it at the vines.

"Fire!" he shouted. "Recoil!"

The vines hissed, recoiling from the heat. They slithered back into the mattress, withering into black dust.

Finriel scrambled out of bed, shivering violently. She fell to the floor, coughing.

Kaelen stood over her. He looked at the frost. He looked at the dead vines.

"Physical manifestation," he said grimly. "Your nightmares are leaking."

"I... I didn't mean to," Finriel wept, hugging herself. "I was dreaming about Thalia."

"You could have strangled yourself," Kaelen said. He grabbed a blanket from an empty bunk and threw it over her. "Or frozen the whole barracks."

He knelt down. "This is getting worse. The leak is becoming a flood."

"I can fix it," Finriel insisted, her teeth chattering. "I just need to focus."

"Focus requires a clear mind," Kaelen said. "Your mind is a storm."

He stood up. "Get dressed. We're going to see Lyra. She has something to show you."

## Part 3: The Truth of Ghosts

Lyra was waiting in the archives—a dusty room filled with scrolls saved from the fall of the old kingdoms.

"Sit," she said, pointing to a chair.

Finriel sat. She was still shivering, despite the blanket.

Lyra placed a crystal sphere on the table. It was cracked, dull.

"This is a Memory Stone," Lyra said. "It belonged to Malakor. Before he fell."

"Why show me this?" Finriel asked.

"Because you need to see the path you are walking," Lyra said. She channeled a tiny amount of mana into the stone.

The crystal flickered. An image appeared.

*A man in shining silver armor stood on a wall. He was handsome, noble. He looked like a hero. He was healing a line of wounded soldiers with golden light.*

*"I can save them all," the man said. His voice was heavy with exhaustion. "I just need more power."*

*The image shifted.*

*The same man, but his armor allowed for grey. His eyes were shadowed. He was standing over a dying demon.*

*"Your power is wasted," the man whispered. He placed a hand on the demon. Violet light flowed.*

*The image shifted again.*

*The man was unrecognizable. His skin was pale, veined with black. He was laughing as he slaughtered a village. Not demons. Humans.*

*"They are just fuel!" he screamed. "I am saving the world! I am the only one strong enough!"*

The image faded.

"That was General Malakor," Lyra said softly. "He started just like you, Finriel. He wanted to save everyone. He couldn't accept that he wasn't strong enough. So he made a deal with the whispers."

"I haven't made a deal," Finriel said quietly. "I just use the energy that is there."

"That *is* the deal," Lyra said. "You think you are using it. But it is using you. It eats your empathy. It eats your restraint. Until the only thing left is the hunger."

Finriel stood up. "I am not him. I am the Chosen One."

"Chosen by who?" Kaelen asked from the doorway. "The Prophecy? Or the Enemy?"

Finriel spun around. "Why do you hate me?!" she screamed. "I am trying! I am fighting! I saved Garret! I am training every day! Why is nothing ever good enough?!"

"Because 'good enough' gets you killed!" Kaelen yelled back. "I am trying to keep you alive!"

"No," Finriel stepped closer, her eyes glowing violet. "You are trying to keep Kaelen alive. You are scared I'll end up like Elfiria. You are seeing ghosts!"

"I AM SEEING A MONSTER IN THE MAKING!" Kaelen roared.

The silence that followed was deafening.

Finriel flinched as if struck.

"A monster," she whispered.

Kaelen’s face crumbled. He reached out. "Fin... I didn't mean..."

"Yes, you did," Finriel backed away. "That's all you see. A monster. A bomb. A problem."

She turned and ran.

"Finriel!" Lyra called.

"Let her go," Kaelen said, sinking into a chair. He put his head in his hands. "Gods... I am failing her."

"You're pushing her," Lyra said gently. "And you're pushing her right into Seccubus's arms."

## Part 4: The Spar

Finriel didn't go to her room. She went to the Pit.

It was empty. Mid-day. The sun was almost bright for once, though the clouds subdued it.

She drew *Dawnbringer*.

"Monster," she whispered. She swung at an invisible enemy.

*He called you a monster,* Aria whispered. *After everything.*

"He's right," Finriel swung again. *Whoosh.* "I almost killed Thalia in my dream. I froze the room."

*That is power leaking because you bottle it up,* Aria explained. *You are a dragon trying to live in a mouse hole. Let it out, little bird. Burn the doubt.*

"Hey! Princess!"

Finriel turned.

Drakon stood on the ridge. The massive Dragon-Kin hopped down, shaking the ground.

"You look like you want to hit something," Drakon rumbled, picking up a massive iron club he used for training. "Care to dance?"

"I might hurt you," Finriel warned. She wasn't bragging. She was terrified she *would*.

"I've got scales thicker than your skull," Drakon grinned, tapping his chest. "Come on. No magic. Just steel. Work out the anger."

Finriel nodded. "Okay."

They circled.

Drakon was fast for his size. He swung the club. Finriel ducked, weaving under the blow. She slashed at his leg. The flat of her blade pinged off his scales.

"Good!" Drakon laughed. "Again!"

They fought for an hour. Sweat poured down Finriel's face. Her muscles burned. It felt good. Clean. Simple.

But then... she slipped.

Drakon swung a backhand. It wasn't meant to connect hard, just to check her. But Finriel was tired. Her foot caught in the gravel.

She fell. The club was coming down.

Panic spiked.

*Defend!*

Finriel didn't use her sword. She threw up her hand.

"STOP!"

She didn't cast a shield. She cast *Gravity*.

Drakon froze mid-swing. His eyes went wide.

The gravity around him increased tenfold. The ground beneath him cracked. He was forced to his knees. The air shimmered violet.

"Fin!" Drakon grunted, straining against the invisible weight. "Let... go!"

Finriel couldn't let go. The power was rushing through her. It felt amazing. She was in control. She was holding a giant with her mind.

*Crush him,* Aria whispered. *Show him who is strong.*

"I... I can't stop!" Finriel panicked. The violet light deepened. Drakon's armor began to buckle. His scales started to crack. He roared in pain.

"FINRIEL!"

A rock hit her in the head.

Finriel gasped, breaking her concentration. The spell shattered.

Drakon collapsed forward, gasping for air.

Kaelen stood on the ridge, another rock in his hand. He looked furious.

Finriel looked at her hands. They were smoking.

"I..." she started.

"Get out," Kaelen said.

"Commander, she didn't mean it," Drakon wheezed, sitting up. "It was an accident."

"GET OUT!" Kaelen roared, pointing at the gate. "Get out of my Pit! You are done for the day!"

Finriel fled.

## Part 5: The Offer

Finriel sat on the ruins of an old watchtower near the edge of the city. It was night. The wind howled.

She was cold. Hungry. Alone.

Thalia hadn't come to find her. No one had.

"I really am a monster," she whispered.

*No,* a voice said. Not inside her head. Beside her.

Finriel jumped up, sword drawn.

A figure stood in the shadows. He was tall, wearing a cloaked hood. But the cloak wasn't fabric. It was shadow.

"Who are you?" Finriel demanded.

The figure lowered the hood.

It was a man. Handsome. Pale. With eyes that swirled like deep pools of violet ink.

"I am a friend," he said smoothly. "I am the one who has been watching over you. The one who gave you the voice."

"Aria?" Finriel asked, confused.

The man smiled. It was a beautiful smile. "I call myself Seccubus. But you can call me Teacher."

Finriel stepped back. "Seccubus. The Demon General. Herald of Whispers."

"Titles," Seccubus waved a hand. "Given by men who fear what they don't understand."

"Kaelen said you would come," Finriel raised her sword. "He said you would try to trick me."

"Did he?" Seccubus took a step forward. Ideally, *Dawnbringer* should have flared. It remained dull. "And does Kaelen understand you? Does he know what it's like to have a sun burning inside your chest that everyone wants to put out?"

Finriel hesitated. "He... he thinks I'm dangerous."

"You *are* dangerous," Seccubus agreed. "That is why you are beautiful. Kaelen wants a soldier. I want a goddess."

He extended a hand.

"Come with me, Finriel. Not to the demons. But to the Truth. Let me show you how to control the power. Let me show you how to save them all without destroying yourself."

"You want to corrupt me," Finriel said.

"I want to *liberate* you," Seccubus corrected. "Look at what the Light has given you. Fear. Isolation. A dead driver. A broken friend."

He gestured to the city below.

"They hate you down there. They mock you. But I... I see you."

Finriel stared at his hand. It was pale. Clean.

*Take it,* Aria whispered. *He can teach us. Just for a little while. Just until we are strong enough to beat him.*

"If I go with you," Finriel said slowly. "Will you teach me how to heal without hurting?"

"I will teach you how to heal the world," Seccubus promised.

Finriel looked back at the city. At the barracks where Thalia was sleeping safely away from her. At the command center where Kaelen was likely drinking to forget her.

She lowered her sword.

"Just until I learn control," she said. "Then I kill you."

Seccubus smiled. It was the smile of a wolf who had just convinced the lamb to open the gate.

"Deal," he said.

He took her hand.

The world dissolved into shadows.
