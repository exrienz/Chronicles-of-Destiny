# Chapter 3: Legends and Ghosts

## Part 1: Echoes of the Past

For Kaelen, sleep was not a respite; it was a battlefield where the enemy always knew his weakness.

*The sky was burning. Not the grey, smoldering burn of the Outer Lands, but a vibrant, impossible violet. The mana-storm screamed, tearing the trees of the Elven forest from their roots.*

*He was twenty-two again. Young. Arrogant. His sword, World-Breaker, felt light in his hands, not the heavy burden it would become. He was laughing.*

*"Come on, El!" he shouted over the wind. "Is that all you've got? I thought High Priestesses were supposed to be scary!"*

*Elfiria stood atop the ridge, her white robes whipping around her. She was laughing too. Her staff glowed with blinding white light, creating a bubble of calm in the chaotic storm. She looked back at him, her silver eyes dancing with mischief.*

*"I'm waiting for you to keep up, Sword Saint!" she teased. "Unless your armor is too heavy?"*

*"I'm just pacing myself!" Kaelen grinned, sprinting up the slope. "We handle the Void-Lord, get back to Aetheria for dinner. I'm buying."*

*"You always buy," Elfiria turned, her expression softening. "Because you know I gave all my coin to the orphanage."*

*"Saint," Kaelen rolled his eyes.*

*"Sinner," she countered.*

*They reached the peak. And there it was. The Void-Gate. A tear in reality, bleeding darkness.*

*But something was wrong. In the memory, Kaelen knew what was coming, but he was powerless to stop it. He was a passenger in his own body.*

*"It's too open," Elfiria said, her smile vanishing. "Kaelen, get back."*

*"No way. We do this together."*

*"Kaelen, NO!"*

*She shoved him. A blast of telekinetic force hit him in the chest, throwing him backward, down the slope. He tumbled, shouting her name. As he looked up, a shadow stepped out of the rift. It wasn't a demon. It was a spear. A single, black spear of condensed void.*

*It didn't aim for him. It aimed for the light.*

*SQUELCH.*

*The sound was small. Insignificant. But it silenced the storm.*

*Kaelen watched, screaming without sound, as the spear pierced Elfiria's chest. The white light of her barrier shattered like glass. She fell. She didn't look at the enemy. She looked at him.*

*She mouthed one word.*

*Live.*

***

Kaelen woke with a gasp, his hand instantly going to the dagger under his pillow.

He was in his quarters in Deephollow. Stone walls. Cold air. The smell of stale sweat and lamp oil.

He sat up, his chest heaving, his heart hammering against his ribs like a trapped bird. He ran a hand over his face, feeling the slick sheen of cold sweat.

"Damn it," he whispered.

He swung his legs out of bed. His knees popped. His back ached. The scar on his chest—where a glancing blow from that same battle had nearly gutted him—throbbed with phantom pain.

He wasn't twenty-two anymore. He was forty. And he was alone.

Kaelen walked to the basin of water on his table. He splashed his face, the freezing water shocking him fully awake. He stared at his reflection in the polished metal mirror. A stranger stared back. Grey hair. Grey beard. A jagged line of scar tissue bisecting a face that had forgotten how to smile.

"You look like hell, old man," he muttered to the reflection.

He dressed slowly, habitually. Padded under-tunic. Chainmail hauberk. Blackened plate pauldrons. Greaves. Finally, he strapped *World-Breaker* to his back. The weight settled on his shoulders, familiar and crushing.

He stepped out into the corridor. It was early, the "sun" barely a lighter shade of grey in the sky.

"Commander!" A sentry snapped to attention.

"At ease," Kaelen grunted. "Where is Lyra?"

"In the map room, sir. With Warlord Drakon."

Kaelen nodded and began the walk. He avoided the east wing of the barracks. He knew who was sleeping there. The girl. The "Chosen One."

Every time he looked at her, he saw Elfiria. Not the face—Elfiria had been softer, older—but the *light*. That damned, naive, blinding light that attracted every monster in the dark.

*I can't do it,* Kaelen thought. *I can't watch another one die.*

But he knew he had no choice.

## Part 2: The Reality of Heroes

Finriel woke up because someone was screaming.

She bolted upright, *Dawnbringer* half-drawn before she realized where she was.

Barracks. Stone walls. Rows of bunks.

The scream was coming from the corner. A soldier, a young man with a bandaged stump where his arm should be, was thrashing in his sleep.

"No! Get back! THE EYES! THE EYES!"

Two other soldiers were holding him down, murmuring soft words.

"Easy, Jace. Easy. You're in Deephollow. You're safe."

"They're eating me! They're eating my fingers!"

Finriel froze, her heart pounding. Thalia was awake too, peering over the edge of the top bunk, her eyes wide with terror.

"Fin?" Thalia whispered.

"It's... it's just a nightmare," Finriel whispered back, though she felt cold all over. Inside the Dome, nightmares were treated with calming tea and therapy crystals. Here, they were just... morning.

The soldier, Jace, eventually settled into sobbing. The others patted his shoulder and went back to their routines as if nothing had happened.

Finriel climbed out of bed. Her beautiful silk dress from the journey was ruined, stained with soot and travel dust. She pulled on her armor—the polished mythril that now looked absurdly shiny in the gloom.

"Hungry?" Thalia asked, holding up a hard biscuit she had scavenged.

"Not really," Finriel said. "But we need strength."

They walked to the mess hall. It was a large cavern filled with long trestle tables. The smell of porridge—grey, lumpy, and smelling faintly of turnips—filled the air.

As Finriel and Thalia entered, the heavy chatter of the room died instantly.

Five hundred soldiers turned to look.

Finriel felt the weight of their gaze. It wasn't the adoring worship she was used to in the Dome. It was curiosity, skepticism, and in some eyes... pity.

"Walk tall," Finriel whispered to herself.

She got a bowl of sludge from the cook, who didn't even look at her, and found a spot at the end of a table. Thalia sat close, practically vibrating with nerves.

"So," a voice rumbled.

Finriel looked up. A massive orc—no, a half-orc, with green skin and tusks jutting from his lower lip—slid onto the bench opposite her. He was missing an ear.

"You're the Savior, huh?" the orc asked. He didn't sound impressed.

"I am Finriel," she said politely.

"I'm Grog," the orc grunted. He pointed his spoon at her shiny armor. "That tin suit is gonna get you killed, Princess. Reflects light. Snipers love it."

"It is Mythril," Finriel defended. "It is enchanted to deflect—"

"Doesn't deflect attention," Grog interrupted. He tapped his own chest, covered in dull, blackened leather. "See this? Demon gut-leather. Tougher than steel, doesn't shine. You wanna live? Ditch the jewelry."

"It is not jewelry," Finriel snapped, her patience fraying. "This armor was crafted by the Master Smiths of Silverglade!"

"Silverglade," Grog spat on the floor. "Place full of flower-sniffers. You ever kill a demon, Princess? A real one? Not those wooden toys you play with?"

"I killed a Void-Scavenger two nights ago," Finriel said, lifting her chin.

The table went quiet. Grog paused, spoon halfway to his mouth.

"A Scavenger?" Grog snorted. Then he laughed. It was a deep, belly-shaking sound. The other soldiers joined in.

"She killed a rat!" one yelled. "Watch out, boys! The Rat-Slayer is here!"

Finriel’s face burned. "It had teeth! It killed our driver!"

"A Scavenger," Grog wiped tears from his eyes, "is what we strip for parts before breakfast. Wait til you meet a Hunter. Or a Mauler. Or..." his face went serious, "a Herald."

*They mock you,* Aria whispered. *Burn them. Show them the light.*

Finriel gripped her spoon so hard it bent. "I am here to help you," she said through gritted teeth.

"We don't need help dying," Grog said, losing his humor. "We need weapons that don't break. You look breakable, Princess. Go back to your Dome before you get blood on that pretty face."

He stood up and walked away. The others followed, leaving Finriel and Thalia alone in a circle of empty space.

"I hate him," Thalia hissed. "I hope he steps on a lego. Do they have legos here?"

"They are right," Finriel said, staring into her grey porridge. "I am shiny. I am loud. I don't fit."

"You'll learn," a new voice said.

Finriel looked up. Standing there was a woman with silver-streaked red hair and eyes that looked like they had seen the end of the world. She wore black leather and moved like a cat.

"Lady Lyra," Finriel recognized her. Part of the Trinity.

"Eat up," Lyra said, not unkindly. "Kaelen wants you in the war room. But first... you need a tour. If you're going to defend this city, you need to know what it smells like."

## Part 3: The Tour of Sorrow

Lyra didn't talk much as she led them through Deephollow. She didn't have to. The city spoke for itself.

She took them to the **Medica-Ward** first.

It wasn't a pristine healing hall like in Silverglade. It was a butcher shop. The smell of rot and disinfectant was overpowering. Healers—magic users whose eyes were sunken with exhaustion—moved between rows of cots.

Finriel saw men with limbs turned black by void-gangrene. She saw a woman whose skin was turning into stone—a curse from a Basilisk demon. She saw children... gods, children... shaking with void-fever.

"We don't have enough mana-crystals to cure the curse," Lyra explained calmly, stopping by the stone-woman. "So we keep her comfortable. She'll be fully statuesque by next week. We'll put her on the wall. A memorial."

Finriel covered her mouth. "You... you just let them die?"

"We prioritize," Lyra said. "Combat effective. Children. Elders. In that order. We save who we can. We mourn the rest."

Next was the **Foundry**.

Dwarves and humans hammered salvage metal. Finriel watched as they melted down a beautiful, ornate Alliance breastplate—likely from a fallen messenger—and beat it into crude, ugly spearheads.

"Why destroy it?" Finriel asked. "That breastplate was art!"

"Art doesn't pierce hide," Lyra said. "We need volume. We need weight. Beauty is a waste of resources here, Finriel."

Finally, Lyra took them to the **Wall**.

They stood on the battlements, looking out over the jagged wasteland. The wind howled.

"That," Lyra pointed to a dark smudge on the horizon, "is the Dead Zone. Nothing grows there. It's where the Demon King's influence is strongest."

She turned to Finriel. "Eighteen years ago, Kaelen and Elfiria stood on a ridge not far from here. They thought they could win a quick war. They were wrong."

Finriel looked at the older woman. "You knew her? Saint Elfiria?"

Lyra’s face tightened. "I was her friend. Before she was a Saint. Before she was a martyr." She looked at Finriel, really looked at her, and her expression softened into something painful. "You look like her. Not the face... but the way you hold yourself. Like the world hasn't broken you yet."

"It won't break me," Finriel promised.

"It broke her," Lyra said softly. "And she was stronger than you. She gave her life to make that Dome. To buy us time. Don't waste it trying to be a hero, Finriel. Just try to be useful."

"I am the Chosen One," Finriel said, the words feeling heavy on her tongue. "I have to be a hero."

Lyra sighed. "That's exactly what she said."

She turned away. "Come. Kaelen is waiting."

## Part 4: The Shadow in the North

The War Room was tense. Drakon was pacing, his tail knocking over a chair. Kaelen sat at the table, staring at a new report.

"It's confirmed?" Kaelen asked without looking up.

"Scouts never lie, Commander," Drakon rumbled. "Not the dead ones."

He tossed a blackened helmet onto the table. It was crushed, imploded from the inside out.

"This was Patrol 4. Found near the Whispering Canyon," Drakon said. "No bodies. just gear. And this."

Kaelen picked up the helmet. He sniffed it. "Lavender?"

"And rot," Drakon nodded. "It's Him. The Herald of Whispers. Seccubus."

Kaelen slammed the helmet down. "Seccubus hasn't been seen in a decade. He operates in the shadows. Why show himself now?"

"Because the bait is here," Lyra said from the doorway.

Finriel stepped in behind her. The room went silent.

Kaelen looked up. His grey eye locked onto Finriel.

"You," he said.

"Me," Finriel answered, trying not to shrink under his gaze.

"Drakon says a Demon General—one of the Seven—is active near our borders for the first time in years," Kaelen said, standing up. "He's hunting something. Or someone."

"You think he's hunting me?" Finriel asked. "Because of the Marks?"

"I think the Demon King smells fresh meat," Kaelen said. He walked around the table, circling her. "You have mana. Lots of it. You're a beacon. As long as you are here, Deephollow is in danger."

"Then I'll leave," Finriel said. "I'll go out and fight him!"

"And die in five minutes," Kaelen snapped. "You don't fight a Herald. You survive them. Seccubus doesn't fight with swords, girl. He fights with your mind. He finds the cracks in your soul and pries them open until you're nothing but a puppet."

Finriel felt a chill run down her spine. *Cracks.* Aria had said something about cracks.

*He lies,* Aria whispered. *He is trying to scare you. He wants to keep you small.*

"I am not afraid of mind games," Finriel said. "My mind is a fortress."

Kaelen laughed. It was a cruel sound.

"A fortress? You've lived in a greenhouse your whole life. You don't know what mental warfare is."

He turned to Drakon. "Double the guard on the perimeter. No one goes in or out without my authorization."

He turned back to Finriel. "And you. You're grounded."

"Grounded?!" Finriel exploded. "I am an Ambassador of the Alliance! You can't command me!"

"In this city, I am God," Kaelen growled, leaning down until they were nose-to-nose. "You want to fight? Fine. Tomorrow morning at dawn. The training pits. You fight me."

"You?" Finriel scoffed. "You have no magic, Commander. I will hurt you."

The room went deadly silent. Lyra closed her eyes. Drakon winced.

Kaelen didn't get angry. He smiled. It was the scariest thing Finriel had ever seen.

"Good," Kaelen whispered. "Try."

## Part 5: The Challenge

That night, Finriel polished *Dawnbringer* until it shone like a mirror.

"Fin," Thalia whispered from her bunk. "Maybe you should apologize. He's... he's really scary."

"He's an old man with a heavy sword," Finriel said, her hands shaking slightly as she worked. "I have the blood of the Ancients. I have the mana of the World Tree. He insulted me. He insulted us."

She looked at her reflection in the blade. Her eyes were bright, feverish.

*Destroy him,* Aria whispered. *Humiliate him. Show the soldiers who the real hero is. Break the old relic and take your rightful place.*

"I will," Finriel whispered back.

"Did you say something?" Thalia asked.

"Just praying," Finriel lied.

She sheathed the sword. Tomorrow, she would show Kaelen. She would show Grog. She would show Lyra.

She wasn't Elfiria. She wasn't a victim.

She was the Chosen One.

And she was going to burn Kaelen's doubt to ash.
