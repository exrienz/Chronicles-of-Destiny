# Chapter 5: The Cost of Healing

## Part 1: The Briefing

The War Room map was bathed in the wavering light of tallow candles. Kaelen and his inner circle stood around it, shadows stretching long against the rough-hewn walls.

"Sector 4 isn't secure," Drakon rumbled, tapping a section of the map marked with a red X. "We lost contact with the mining outpost yesterday. Could be comms failure. Could be a tunneler beast."

"We need that iron," Lyra said, her voice tight. "The foundry is running on fumes. If we don't get a shipment by week's end, we can't repair the gate fortifications."

Kaelen stared at the map. He looked tired. More tired than usual. The dark circles under his eyes spoke of nights spent patrolling the walls instead of sleeping.

"I'll take a squad," Kaelen said. "We'll sweep the tunnels."

"No," Lyra countered. "You're needed here. The West Wall is showing stress fractures. If Malakor hits us there while you're playing miner, we lose the city."

"Then who?" Kaelen asked. "Grog is on the North Ridge. Silas is wounded."

"Send the girl," Drakon said.

The room went quiet.

Kaelen looked up. "Finriel?"

"She's ready, Kaelen," Drakon said, crossing his massive arms. "I've seen her in the Pit. She's fast. She's lethal. And let's be honest—she has more raw firepower than any ten of us combined. If it is a tunneler, she can glass it."

"She's green," Kaelen argued. "She's pit-trained, not field-tested. There's a difference between fighting Grog and fighting a void-stalker in the dark."

"She has to learn sometime," Lyra added quietly. "And Sector 4 is low-risk. It's close to the perimeter. Send a veteran squad with her. Let her stretch her legs."

Kaelen hesitated. He looked at the map, then at the door where he knew Finriel was waiting.

He hated it. Every instinct in his gut screamed *unsafe*. But Lyra was right. They were stretched too thin.

"Fine," Kaelen growled. "But she doesn't lead. Sergeant Vick leads. She follows orders. If she steps out of line, Vick drags her back by her ear."

He paused. "And send Thalia."

Lyra raised an eyebrow. "The handmaiden? She's a civilian."

"She's an anchor," Kaelen said, rubbing his scar. "The girl... Finriel... she drifts sometimes. I've seen it in the Pit. She gets this look in her eyes. The friend keeps her grounded. Give the handmaiden a crossbow and keep her in the rear."

"Understood," Lyra nodded.

"Call them in."

## Part 2: The Departure

Finriel stood at the South Gate, adjusting the straps of her armor. It was no longer the shiny, pristine mythril that had arrived weeks ago. It was scuffed, darkened with soot, and dented in places. She wore a heavy grey cloak over it, the hood pulled up to hide her ears.

She looked like an Exile.

"You nervous?" Thalia asked. She was wearing a borrowed leather brigandine that was slightly too big for her and clutching a light crossbow like it was a venomous snake she was afraid might bite her.

"No," Finriel lied. Her stomach was doing backflips. "It's just a recon mission. In and out."

"That's what they always say in the stories," Thalia muttered. "Then the dragon wakes up."

"There are no dragons," Finriel touched the hilt of *Dawnbringer*. "Ignisar hasn't been seen in centuries. It's just demons."

"Just demons," Thalia rolled her eyes. "That's comforting."

Sergeant Vick walked up to them. He was a man made of gristle and scars, missing three fingers on his left hand. He smoked a foul-smelling pipe.

"Listen up, Princess," Vick grunted, blowing smoke in Finriel's face. She didn't cough. "You're heavy artillery. That means you stay behind the shield wall until I tell you to fire. You don't charge. You don't try to be a hero. You point and shoot where I say. Got it?"

"Yes, Sergeant," Finriel said.

"And you," Vick pointed his pipe at Thalia. "You stay with the mules. If you see anything with more than two eyes, scream."

"I'm very good at screaming," Thalia promised.

"Move out!"

The squad—twelve hardened veterans, Finriel, and Thalia—marched out of the gate and into the grey wasteland.

The journey to Sector 4 took fours hours. The landscape was a nightmare of twisted rock formations and sulfur vents. The ground crunched underfoot—bone-dust and shale.

Finriel walked in the center of the formation. Her senses were dialed up to eleven. She could feel the mana currents in the air. They were sluggish here, corrupted.

*Can you feel them?* Aria whispered. *They are watching.*

"Who?" Finriel thought back.

*The shadows. They are hungry. They smell your light.*

Finriel gripped her staff. "Vick," she whispered. "Something's watching us."

Vick held up a hand. The squad froze instantly, dropping into combat crouches.

"Sector?" Vick hissed.

"Everywhere," Finriel said, her eyes glowing faintly green. "Underneath."

The ground exploded.

## Part 3: The Ambush

It wasn't a tunneler beast. It was a swarm.

Hook-Horrors—insectoid demons the size of ponies with scythe-like claws—erupted from the shale. Dozens of them. They screeched, a sound like grinding metal.

"SHIELDS!" Vick roared.

The veterans slammed their shields together, forming a circle. Thalia shrieked, scrambling into the center with the mules.

"Contact front!"

A Hook-Horror slammed into a shield, its claws sparking against the iron. The soldier grunted, bracing his weight against the impact.

"Hold the line!" Vick shouted, hacking at a claw with his axe. "Princess! Fire mission! North quadrant! 30 meters!"

Finriel stepped forward, raising her staff.

*Burn them,* Aria hissed.

"Ignis... MAXIMA!"

Finriel didn't just cast a fireball. She unleashed a torrent. A river of white-hot flame poured from her staff, engulfing the northern side of the ambush. The Hook-Horrors screeched as their chitin melted. The smell of burning ozone and demon ichor filled the air.

"Holy shit," a soldier muttered.

"Don't gawk! Kill the stragglers!" Vick ordered.

But the swarm wasn't stopping. More erupted from the south. From the east.

"They're flanking!" Finriel shouted. She spun, *Dawnbringer* in her other hand.

A Horror leaped over the shield wall, landing in the center of the circle—right next to Thalia.

"Thalia!" Finriel screamed.

Thalia raised her crossbow, eyes squeezed shut, and pulled the trigger. The bolt hit the demon in the shoulder. It just pissed it off. The Horror raised a scythe-claw.

Time slowed.

Finriel couldn't reach her. She was too far. The soldiers were engaged.

*Use the Reach,* Aria commanded. *The Void binds all.*

Finriel didn't hesitate. She threw her hand out. But she didn't call for roots. She didn't call for wind.

She reached for the *space* between them.

The air around the Horror warped. Gravity twisted.

*CRUNCH.*

The demon imploded. It was as if a giant, invisible fist had simply crushed it into a ball of gore. Black blood splattered Thalia’s face.

Thalia stood there, shaking, staring at the pile of meat that had been a monster a second ago.

"Fin?" she whispered.

Finriel lowered her hand. Her fingers were smoking. Violet smoke.

"I... I got it," Finriel breathed.

"Focus!" Vick yelled. "They're retreating!"

The remaining Horrors, sensing the sudden spike in power, chittered and burrowed back into the earth. Within seconds, the wasteland was silent again, save for the groans of the wounded.

"Sound off!" Vick called.

"Clear."
"Clear."
"Garret's down! He's gutted!"

Finriel ran to where Garret—the young scout from the first day—was lying. His armor was torn open. Three deep gashes ran across his stomach. Blood was pulsing out, dark and fast.

"He's bleeding out!" the medic shouted, pressing bandages to the wound. "I can't stop it! It's too deep!"

Garret’s face was grey. His eyes were unfocused. "Mom?" he mumbled. "It's cold..."

"Let me," Finriel said, dropping to her knees. "I can heal him. I have High Druid training."

"Do it fast," Vick said, grim-faced. "We have to move before they come back."

Finriel placed her hands on the wound. She closed her eyes.

*Mend.*

She reached for the light mana within her. The golden, warm mana she had used a thousand times to heal scraped knees and training bruises.

But the mana wasn't there.

Or rather, it was blocked. The corruption of the wasteland was choking her connection to nature. She grasped for the light, but it slipped away like water.

"I... I can't," she panicked. "The mana... it's too thin here."

Garret coughed, blood bubbling on his lips. "Please..."

"He's dying, Finriel!" Thalia cried from behind her.

*Don't use the air,* Aria whispered. *Use the life around you. It is plentiful.*

*What?*

*To give live, you must take life. Equivalent exchange. Take it from the source. He has plenty of memories. Plenty of spirit. Use what is there to fix what is broken.*

It didn't make sense. But Garret was dying. His heartbeat was a flutter under her palm.

Finriel was desperate. She didn't want another driver. She didn't want another pyre.

"Live," she commanded.

And she pulled.

She didn't pull from the earth. She pulled from *Garret*. She grabbed the energy of his mind, his soul, and twisted it, forcing it into his flesh.

It felt... wrong. It felt cold. Sticky.

Violet light pulsed under her hands.

Garret’s eyes snapped wide open. He screamed.

It wasn't a scream of pain. It was a scream of absolute, existential terror. It was the sound of a man watching his soul be unraveled.

"NO! DON'T TAKE IT! DON'T TAKE HER!" Garret shrieked.

"Hold him down!" Vick yelled.

"Almost... almost..." Finriel gritted her teeth. She felt memories rushing through her fingers—a mother’s face, the smell of baking bread, a first kiss, a childhood dog. She felt them... and she *burned* them. She used them as fuel to stitch the flesh back together.

The wounds closed. The skin knit itself seamless, leaving a scar that looked like a burn brand.

Garret stopped screaming. He went limp.

Finriel pulled her hands back, gasping. She felt dizzy. Nauseous.

"He's stable," the medic checked the pulse. "Heartbeat strong. bleeding stopped. That was... that was fast, Lady Finriel."

Finriel looked at her hands. They were trembling. "I saved him."

"Good work," Vick clapped her shoulder. "Pack him up. let's move."

Thalia walked up to Finriel. She wasn't looking at Garret. She was looking at Finriel.

"Fin," Thalia whispered. "His eyes."

Finriel looked down at Garret.

His eyes were open. He was breathing. But there was nothing there. No recognition. No pain. No fear. Just... blankness.

"Garret?" Finriel asked. "Can you hear me?"

Garret just stared at the sky.

"He's in shock," the medic said, lifting him onto a stretcher. "He'll come around."

*He won't,* Aria whispered, sounding satisfied. *You saved the body, little bird. The cost was fair.*

Finriel stood up, wiping her hands on her cloak violently, trying to get the feeling of his memories off her skin.

"Let's go," she said, her voice shaking. "I want to go back."

As they marched back towards Deephollow, Finriel couldn't stop looking at Garret’s empty face. And deep inside, buried under the guilt, she felt something else.

Power.

The "healing" hadn't drained her. It had *fed* her. She felt stronger than she had all day.

And that terrified her more than any demon.

## Part 4: The Report

Kaelen listened to Vick’s report in silence.

"Ambush was repelled. Zero casualties. One wounded—Garret—stabilized by Lady Finriel. Mission successful. We secured the mine entrance."

"And Garret?" Kaelen asked. "How is he?"

"Catatonic, sir," Vick admitted. "Healers say he's physically fine. Wounds closed up like magic. But he... he doesn't speak. Doesn't seem to know his own name."

Kaelen looked at Lyra. Lyra looked pale.

"Dismissed," Kaelen said.

When Vick left, Kaelen poured a drink. Whisky. Cheap stuff.

"She healed him," Kaelen said. "And she wiped him."

"It's a Void-Healing technique," Lyra whispered. "I've read about it in the forbidden archives. Transference of essence. It’s... it’s dark magic, Kaelen. Necromancy adjacent."

"She didn't know," Kaelen defended, though he didn't sound convinced. "She panicked."

"Or," Drakon rumbled from the corner, "she is tapping into something she shouldn't. Her mana signature when she returned... it was spiking. Violet spikes."

Kaelen slammed the glass down. "She's a child! She doesn't know what she's doing!"

"She's dangerous," Lyra said. "Kaelen, if she turns... if she falls..."

"She won't fall," Kaelen said. "I'll train her harder. I'll teach her control. I'll make her so tired she can't listen to whatever whispers she's hearing."

He walked to the window, looking out at the barracks.

"She saved him," Kaelen said. "In her mind, she's a hero. If I punish her for saving a life, I lose her. I have to guide her."

"Guide her where?" Lyra asked.

"Away from the dark," Kaelen said.

But as he watched the flickering lights of the city, he wondered if it was already too late. Because Elfiria had started hearing whispers too. Before the end.

And look how that turned out.
