# Chapter 10: The Truth from Chains

## Part 1: The Invitation

Three days had passed since the Stalker incident. Finriel lived in the crypts now. She was comfortable in the dark.

She practiced the Soul-Weaving. She healed rats. Then she drained them. Then she healed them again.

It was... intoxicating.

"Fin?"

Finriel looked up from her scroll. A small beam of light cut through the gloom of the crypt entrance.

Thalia stood there. She looked terrified, clutching a lantern as if it were a weapon.

"Thalia?" Finriel stood up, hiding the scroll behind her back. "How did you find me?"

"I... I followed the rats," Thalia whispered, looking at the dozens of cages Finriel had set up. "Fin... what are you doing?"

"Experiments," Finriel said. "I'm learning how to fix Garret. Look."

She pointed to a rat. It was missing a tail. Finriel waved her hand. Violet light pulsed. The tail grew back.

"See?" Finriel smiled. "No memory cost. I figured it out. You just have to use... ambient life force. Moss. Bugs. It's efficient."

Thalia looked at the rat. Then she looked at the moss on the wall, which was brown and dead in a perfect circle.

"It's beautiful, Fin," Thalia lied. Her voice shook.

"Why are you here?" Finriel asked, stepping closer. "Did Kaelen send you?"

Thalia flinched. "He... he wants to talk. He says he was wrong."

"Wrong?" Finriel laughed. "Kaelen never admits he's wrong."

"He looked... broken, Fin," Thalia stepped forward. "He said he pushed you too hard. He said he wants to start over. He prepared a feast. In the Great Hall. Just the three of us. No guards. No lectures."

"A feast?" Finriel’s stomach growled. She had been eating rats. "Real food?"

"Roast chicken," Thalia promised. "And moon-berry tarts. I made them myself."

Finriel hesitated. She looked at the scroll. Seccubus wasn't here. He had vanished yesterday, saying he had 'preparations' to make.

"I miss you, Fin," Thalia whispered. "Please come home."

Finriel looked at her best friend. At the tear tracking down Thalia's dirty cheek.

The anger in her chest softened. Just a little.

"Okay," Finriel breathed. "One dinner. If he lectures me, I leave."

"Deal," Thalia smiled weakly. "Deal."

## Part 2: The Feast

The Great Hall of Deephollow was usually a bustling mess hall. Tonight, it was empty.

A single long table was set in the center. Candles—hundreds of them—provided a warm, golden light.

There was roast chicken. There were tarts. There was wine.

Kaelen stood at the head of the table. He wasn't wearing armor. He wore formal robes of deep blue—Alliance colors.

"Finriel," he nodded. "Thank you for coming."

Finriel stopped ten paces away. She kept her hood up. "Thalia said you wanted to talk."

"I do," Kaelen gestured to a chair. "Please. Eat."

Finriel sat. Thalia sat next to her. Kaelen sat opposite.

Finriel reached for a tart. She took a bite. It was sweet. It tasted like home. She closed her eyes, savouring it.

"I'm sorry," Kaelen said.

Finriel opened her eyes. "Excuse me?"

"I'm sorry," Kaelen repeated. He poured wine. "I compared you to Malakor. That was unfair. Malakor was a grown man who chose power. You are... you were thrust into this."

"I am handling it," Finriel said, taking a sip of wine. It was good. But... it tasted slightly bitter. Herbs?

"I see that," Kaelen said looking at Thalia. Thalia was staring at her plate, not eating. "You killed a Shadowstalker. The people love you."

"They feel safe," Finriel said. "That's what matters."

"Is it?" Kaelen leaned forward. "Safety at the cost of your soul? Finriel... Lyra told me about the violet flame. Do you know what that is?"

"It's Void mana," Finriel said. "It's just another color, Kaelen."

"It's corruption," Kaelen said softly. "It alters the mind. It makes you paranoid. Aggressive. Have you felt it? The anger?"

Finriel felt a spike of irritation. "I am not angry. I am focused."

"Are you?" Kaelen asked. "Then why are you trembling?"

Finriel looked at her hands. They were shaking. Her vision blurred slightly.

"What..." she mumbled. "What is in this wine?"

She tried to stand up. Her legs gave way. She slumped back into the chair.

"Thalia?" Finriel looked at her friend.

Thalia was crying silently. She wouldn't look at Finriel.

"I'm sorry," Thalia whispered. "He said it would save you."

"Save me?" Finriel slurred. She tried to summon the Void. To blast the table.

But her mana was gone. The herbs. Mana-bane root.

"It’s not a poison," Kaelen said, standing up. "It’s a suppressor. Powerful enough to knock out a dragon."

He signaled.

From the shadows of the hall, twelve hooded figures emerged. Assasins? No. Mages. Wearing robes covered in binding runes.

"Kaelen," Finriel gasped, trying to reach for *Dawnbringer*. Her arm felt like lead. "Don't..."

"I can't let you fall, Finriel," Kaelen said sadly. "I promised Elfiria I would protect the Light. Even if I have to break the vessel to save it."

He nodded to the mages.

"Bind her."

## Part 3: The Ritual

Finriel woke up screaming.

She was suspended in the air, chains of glowing white light wrapped around her wrists and ankles. She was in the center of the Great Hall.

Underneath her was a massive runic circle drawn in salt and silver dust.

The twelve mages stood around the circle, chanting.

*Puritas. Lux. Sanctus.*

Pain. Pure, white-hot pain coursed through her body. The chains weren't just holding her; they were burning her. They were searching inside her soul, hunting for the darkness.

"GET IT OUT!" Finriel shrieked. "STOP!"

Kaelen stood outside the circle, watching. His face was pale.

"Endure it, Finriel," he called out. "It's burning the corruption. It has to hurt."

"IT'S BURNING ME!" Finriel yelled.

Inside her mind, the violet flame was fighting back. It hissed and roared as the Light tried to extinguish it.

*They are killing us!* Aria screamed. *They are cutting us out!*

"I won't let them!" Finriel thought.

She struggled. The chains tightened.

"Increase the flow!" Kaelen ordered. "She's fighting it!"

The mages chanted louder. The white light intensified.

It felt like being skinned alive. Finriel felt memories surfacing—the rat she healed, the Stalker she killed, the power she felt.

And then... she felt them slipping away.

The Light was erasing the Void. It was resetting her. Making her "pure" again.

Making her weak.

"No..." she moaned. "That's mine..."

"It's a parasite!" Kaelen yelled. "Let it go!"

"NO!"

Finriel looked at Thalia. Thalia was standing by Kaelen, horrified. Hands over her mouth.

"Thalia, help me!" Finriel begged.

Thalia took a step forward. "Kaelen... she's dying! Look at her skin!"

Finriel's skin was smoking.

"It's the only way," Kaelen insisted, holding Thalia back. "We have to cleanse her."

Finriel saw the look in Kaelen's eyes. It wasn't hate. It was love. Twisted, desperate, fanatical love. He loved the *idea* of her. The Lightbringer.

He hated the *reality* of her.

Something snapped inside Finriel.

It wasn't the chains. It was the hope. The hope that she could ever please him. The hope that she could belong here.

*He betrayed you,* Seccubus's voice rose from the depths, louder than the chanting. *He poisoned you. He chained you. He is torturing you.*

*Break him.*

"I..." Finriel whispered.

She stopped fighting the pain. She embraced it. She took the agony of the white fire and she fed it to the Void.

*Fuel.*

The violet flame inside her didn't go out. It drank the light. It turned it purple.

"What is she doing?" a mage gasped. "The mana... it's reversing!"

"Stop the ritual!" Kaelen shouted. "Abort!"

"Too late," Finriel whispered.

She opened her eyes. They were violet suns.

"MY TURN."

She screamed.

It wasn't a spell. It was an explosion.

A shockwave of pure, corrupted mana erupted from her body.

The white chains turned black. Then they shattered.

The backlash hit the mages. They were thrown back like ragdolls, their robes catching fire.

The runic circle cracked. The floor exploded.

Kaelen threw himself over Thalia, shielding her with his body as bits of stone shrapnel peppered the hall.

Silence fell.

Dust swirled in the air.

Finriel landed on the floor. She stood up. Her clothes were burnt rags. Her skin was marked with glowing violet veins.

She looked at Kaelen.

"You tried to erase me," she said. Her voice echoed.

Kaelen stood up, drawing *World-Breaker*. "I tried to save you!"

"You tried to make me into Elfiria," Finriel stepped forward. The floor cracked under her feet. "But Elfiria is dead. I am Finriel."

She raised a hand. Black fire pooled in her palm.

"And I am done being your puppet."

"Fin, no!" Thalia screamed, pulling away from Kaelen. "Don't!"

Finriel looked at Thalia.

"You led me here," Finriel whispered. "You helped him."

"I... I thought..." Thalia sobbed.

"You thought I was broken," Finriel said. "Everyone thinks I'm broken."

She clenched her fist. The black fire vanished.

She looked at Kaelen.

"I won't kill you," she said. "Because you're already dead, old man. You died eighteen years ago on that hill."

She turned and walked towards the door.

"Finriel!" Kaelen shouted. "If you walk out that door, you are an enemy of the Alliance! I will hunt you!"

Finriel stopped. She looked over her shoulder.

"Come and try."

She blasted the doors off their hinges and walked out into the night.

## Part 4: The Fall

Finriel walked through the city. Soldiers saw her and ran. The violet aura around her was suffocating.

She didn't go to the crypts. She went to the North Gate.

The guards there—Grog and his squad—blocked her path.

"Princess," Grog said, looking nervous. "Command says you're a rogue. Orders are to detain."

Finriel looked at Grog. The orc who had mocked her.

"Move, Grog," she said.

"Can't do that," Grog raised his axe. "Kaelen's orders."

Finriel sighed. "I liked you, Grog."

She didn't use a spell. She used *Shadow Step*.

She appeared in front of him. She placed a hand on his chest.

*Sleep.*

She pumped a massive burst of sedative mana into his heart. Grog’s eyes rolled back. He collapsed.

The other guards raised their spears.

"Anyone else want a nap?" Finriel asked.

They dropped their spears and backed away.

Finriel walked out of the gate. Out of Deephollow.

She walked into the wasteland. The wind howled, but she didn't feel the cold. The void kept her warm.

She walked for a mile until the lights of the city were just a smudge.

"Seccubus?" she called out.

The shadows coalesced. The demon appeared. He was smiling. A genuine, delighted smile.

"That," Seccubus said, "was magnificent."

"I broke the chains," Finriel said. She felt hollow. Numb.

"You broke the past," Seccubus corrected. "Now... we build the future."

"Where do we go?" Finriel asked.

"To my Spire," Seccubus pointed north. "To the Heart of Whispers. There, I will teach you the final secret."

"What secret?"

"How to bring the dead back," Seccubus said. "Fully back. No cost."

Finriel’s heart skipped a beat. "You mean Thalia? If... if she dies?"

"I mean anyone," Seccubus said. "Even Elfiria."

Finriel froze. "Elfiria?"

"Imagine," Seccubus whispered, leaning in. "If you brought back Kaelen's precious Saint. He would have to forgive you. He would have to love you. You would be the ultimate hero."

It was a lie. A beautiful, terrible lie.

But Finriel was desperate. She wanted to fix what she had broken. She wanted Kaelen to look at her with pride, not fear.

"Teach me," she said.

"Come," Seccubus offered his hand.

Finriel took it.

And together, they walked into the deep dark.
