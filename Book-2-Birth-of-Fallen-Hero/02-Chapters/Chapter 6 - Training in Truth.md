# Chapter 6: Training in Truth

## Part 1: The Silence of Garret

The Medica-Ward was quiet, save for the low moans of the dying.

Finriel sat by Garret’s bed. He was awake. His eyes were open, staring at a stain on the ceiling. He hadn't blinked in two minutes.

"Garret?" Finriel whispered.

She reached out and touched his hand. It was warm. His pulse was steady. The horrific gashes that had torn his stomach open were gone, replaced by smooth, pale skin marked with a faint, violet scar that looked like a burn.

"Garret, it's me. Finriel."

Nothing. No recognition. No fear. Just... emptiness.

"He hasn't spoken," the duty healer whispered, stepping up behind her. "He eats if we spoon feed him. He drinks if we pour it in his mouth. But... the lights are on, and no one is home."

Finriel pulled her hand back as if burned.

"I saved him," she said, her voice trembling. "His body... it was ruined. I fixed it."

"You did," the healer agreed, though her eyes were wary. "But memories are tied to the soul, Lady Finriel. When you pulled... whatever you pulled... you might have pulled too hard."

Finriel stood up. "I did what I had to do."

"Of course," the healer bowed, but she stepped back, putting distance between them. "We are grateful. A living husk is better than a corpse. We can... retrain him. Maybe."

Finriel looked at Garret one last time.

*He is peaceful,* Aria whispered. *No more fear. No more pain. You gave him a gift, little bird.*

"It feels like theft," Finriel thought.

*Survival always is.*

Finriel turned and walked out. She passed Thalia in the hallway. Thalia was carrying a basket of bandages. She stopped when she saw Finriel.

"Hey," Thalia said. Her smile was tight. Forced.

"Hey," Finriel tried to smile back. "How is he?"

"The same," Finriel said. She reached out to squeeze Thalia's arm, but Thalia flinched. It was subtle—a tiny jerk of the shoulder—but Finriel felt it like a slap.

"Sorry," Thalia stammered, backing away. "Static shock. Dry air. You know."

"Yeah," Finriel lowered her hand. "Static."

Thalia hurried past her.

Finriel watched her go. A cold knot formed in her stomach. Thalia was afraid of her. Everyone was afraid of her.

*Let them be afraid,* Aria purred. *Fear is respect. And you need respect if you are going to lead.*

"I don't want to lead," Finriel whispered to the empty stone corridor. "I just want to be normal."

*Too late.*

## Part 2: The Sanctuary

"You're late."

Kaelen stood in the center of the training ring. But not the gritty Pit of Deephollow. This was a place Finriel hadn't seen before.

Kaelen had led her up a winding stair cut into the canyon wall, high above the smog line. Here, there was a small plateau. A single, twisted tree grew from the rock—a Ghost-Willow, its leaves translucent white. Under the tree sat a simple stone meditation circle.

The air here was cleaner. Thinner.

"I was visiting Garret," Finriel said defensively, stepping into the circle.

Kaelen’s face hardened. "And? Did he ask about the weather?"

Finriel looked away. "He's alive."

"Is he?" Kaelen asked. He walked around her, his wooden sword resting on his shoulder. "Breathing isn't living, Finriel. A golem breathes if you enchant it right. A zombie walks."

"I am not a necromancer!" Finriel snapped, her mana flaring. Green sparks mixed with violet wisps.

"Then stop casting like one!" Kaelen roared.

He swung the wooden sword. Hard.

Finriel brought her staff up to block. *CLACK.* The impact vibrated down her arms.

"You tapped into the Void," Kaelen accused, pressing the attack. *Swing. Block. Swing. Block.* "I felt it. Lyra felt it. You used Equivalent Exchange. That is forbidden magic!"

"He was dying!" Finriel screamed, parrying a thrust and riposting with a wind-blast. "The Light wasn't working! The Dome's magic is weak out here!"

"So you switched sides?" Kaelen swept her legs.

Finriel jumped, floating for a second on a cushion of air. "I used what was available! I adapted! Isn't that what you wanted? Survival?"

"Survival implies you stay *you*," Kaelen jumped up, grabbing her ankle and slamming her back to the ground. She hit the dust hard.

He stood over her, breathing heavily.

"The Void is not a tool, Finriel," he said, his voice low and dangerous. "It is a creditor. It lends you power, yes. But the interest rate is your soul."

Finriel pushed herself up, spitting dirt. "I can control it."

"No one controls it!" Kaelen yelled, losing his composure. "Elfiria thought she could control it! She thought she could use the enemy's power against them! And do you know what happened?"

Finriel froze. "She... she died saving the world."

"She died," Kaelen whispered, "because she hesitated. Because the Void whispered to her that she was a god, and for one second... she believed it."

He dropped his sword. He looked old. Incredible, anciently old.

"Sit," he commanded.

Finriel sat.

"Close your eyes."

She closed them.

"Reach for your light," Kaelen guided. "Not the power you feel out here. The power you brought with you. The seed of the World Tree. Find it."

Finriel reached inward. She pushed past the cold, sticky whispers of Aria. She pushed past the anger. Deep, deep down, she saw it. A tiny spark of gold.

It was small. So small compared to the ocean of grey around it.

"It feels... weak," Finriel whispered.

"It is quiet," Kaelen corrected. "But it is real. The Light doesn't scream, Finriel. It endures. It’s the sun rising after a storm. It’s the grass growing through the concrete. It doesn't destroy. It reveals."

"Reveal what?" Finriel asked.

"The truth," Kaelen said. "Look at Garret with that light. What do you see?"

Finriel focused on the memory of Garret. She tried to bathe him in the golden light.

The image shifted. She didn't see the healed wound. She saw... torn threads. She saw the spiritual scars of what she had done. She saw the memories she had burned—images of his mother, his laughter—turning to ash.

She gasped, her eyes snapping open.

"I hurt him," she realized. Horror washed over her. "I... I mutilated his soul."

"Yes," Kaelen said softly.

Finriel burst into tears. Real tears. Not the angry tears of the duel. These were tears of guilt.

"I didn't mean to," she sobbed. "I just wanted to save him."

Kaelen knelt beside her. He didn't hug her. He wasn't that kind of man anymore. But he put a hand on her shoulder.

"I know," he said. "That is why we train. So you never have to make that choice again."

He stood up. "Meditate here. Until the sun goes down. cleanse the taint. If you feel the Violet again... you push it out. understood?"

"Yes, Commander," Finriel whispered.

Kaelen walked away, descending the stairs.

Finriel sat under the Ghost-Willow. She tried to meditate. She tried to hold onto the golden spark.

But it was so hard. The silence was loud.

*He lies,* Aria whispered.

"Go away," Finriel mentally screamed. "You made me hurt him!"

*I made you save him. Would you prefer him dead? Would you prefer to explain to his mother that you let him die because you were afraid of a little darkness?*

"I destroyed his memories!"

*Memories are the past. Life is the future. You traded the past for the future. It is a noble trade.*

Finriel wavered. Aria made sense. Cruel sense, but sense.

*Kaelen is afraid,* Aria continued. *He failed Elfiria. Now he wants to make you weak so he can protect you. He wants to put you in a cage. A cage of 'morality'.*

*But the Enemy has no morality. To beat a monster... you must be a bigger monster.*

Finriel looked at her hand. She could summon the Light. It was warm. Safe. Weak.

Or she could summon the Void.

She closed her eyes. She touched the Light... and then, just to see if she could... she touched the Void.

The rush was instant. Intoxicating.

*See?* Aria purred. *Why walk when you can fly?*

Finriel opened her eyes. The sun was setting. The Ghost-Willow cast long, skeletal shadows.

She stood up. She felt... different.

She hadn't pushed the Violet out. She had tucked it away. Deep down. Just in case.

Because Kaelen was right—Garret was a husk. But Aria was also right—he was *alive*.

And alive was better than dead.

Wasn't it?

## Part 3: The Wedge

That evening, the mess hall was loud. Celebrating.

"To Garret!" a soldier toasted. "The lucky bastard!"

Finriel sat alone. Thalia hadn't come to dinner.

"Mind if I sit?"

It was Lyra.

Finriel nodded.

Lyra sat down, placing a bowl of stew on the table. "Kaelen told me about the session at the Sanctuary. He thinks you made a breakthrough."

"He thinks a lot of things," Finriel muttered.

Lyra studied her. "He cares, Finriel. I know he seems like a rock with a grudge, but he sees potential in you. He just doesn't want you to burn out."

"I'm not burning out," Finriel said, stabbing a potato. "I'm evolving."

Lyra froze. "Evolving?"

"The world here is different," Finriel said, regurgitating Aria’s logic. "The magic is different. Why should I fight with one hand tied behind my back? Why limit myself to 'Light' when the Void is right there?"

Lyra leaned in, her voice low. "Because the Void has a consciousness, Finriel. It isn't just energy. It's Him. The Demon King. Every time you use it, you open a door. And one day, you won't be able to close it."

"I can handle it," Finriel said.

"That's what the First General said," Lyra countered. "Malakor. He was a Paladin once. Did you know that? He thought he could use the enemy's weapons. Now he's a monster who wears human skin."

Finriel stood up. "I'm not Malakor. I'm the Chosen One."

"Being Chosen doesn't make you immune to corruption," Lyra said sadly. "It just makes you a bigger prize."

Finriel stormed out of the mess hall. She was sick of lectures. Sick of fear.

She went to the barracks.

Thalia wasn't in her bunk.

"Where is she?" Finriel asked a soldier.

"Moved," the soldier said, without looking up from his dice game. "Asked the Sergeant for a transfer to the logistics quarters. Said the snoring was keeping her up."

Finriel felt a punch to the gut. Thalia didn't care about snoring.

She had moved to get away from *her*.

Finriel walked to the logistics wing. She found Thalia’s new bunk. It was in a small room shared with three washer-women.

Thalia was there, folding clothes.

"Thalia?"

Thalia jumped, dropping a shirt. She turned around. Her eyes were wide. Fearful.

"Oh. Hi, Fin."

"Why did you move?" Finriel asked, her voice cracking. "We... we always room together."

"It's just... closer to the laundry," Thalia lied. She was a terrible liar. "And... you know. You need your rest. For training."

"You're scared of me," Finriel said.

Thalia looked down. "I saw what you did to Garret, Fin. I saw his eyes. It... it wasn't you. It was something else."

"It saved his life!" Finriel yelled. "Why does no one care about that?!"

"At what cost?" Thalia whispered. "He doesn't remember his own mother, Fin. Is that life?"

"It's better than being dead!"

"Is it?" Thalia looked up, tears in her eyes. "If I get hurt, Fin... promise me. Promise me you won't do that to me. I'd rather die than be empty."

Finriel stared at her best friend. The only person who had ever loved her just for being Fin.

"I..."

*Promise her,* Aria sneered. *Lies are easy.*

"I promise," Finriel said.

Thalia let out a breath she had been holding. She stepped forward and hugged Finriel. It was a stiff hug. Hesitant.

"I miss the garden," Thalia whispered into Finriel's shoulder. "I miss the sun."

"Me too," Finriel lied. She didn't miss the sun. The sun felt weak now.

"Go to sleep, Thal," Finriel said, pulling away. "I'll see you tomorrow."

"Okay. Night, Fin."

Finriel walked back to the empty barracks. She lay in her bunk, staring at the stone ceiling.

She was alone. Truly alone.

Kaelen feared her. Lyra pitied her. Thalia was terrified of her.

*You have me,* Aria whispered. *I am never afraid. I am never judging. I am always here.*

Finriel closed her eyes.

"I know," she thought. "What do we do next?"

*Tomorrow,* Aria promised, *We stop playing defense. Tomorrow... we hunt.*

Finriel smiled in the darkness. Her marks glowed.

Not green.

Violet.
