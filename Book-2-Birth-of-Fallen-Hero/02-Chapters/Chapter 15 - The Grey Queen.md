# Chapter 15: The Grey Queen

## Part 1: The Return

The Southern Gate of Silverglade was the most heavily fortified position in the entire Holy Dome perimeter. It was a marvel of magic and engineering—white stone walls rising two hundred feet, reinforced with mana-batteries that could vaporize a dragon.

Captain Valerius stood on the ramparts, looking out at the wasteland.

"Quiet today," his lieutenant said.

"Too quiet," Valerius muttered. "The sensors are flat. No demon activity for fifty miles."

"maybe they gave up," the lieutenant joked.

"Demons don't give up. They regroup."

Valerius raised his binoculars. He scanned the horizon.

He saw a speck.

"Movement," he called out. "South-South-West. Single target."

"A scout?"

"Too big for a scout. Too small for a Siege-Beast."

The speck grew closer. It was a rider.

"It's... a woman," Valerius lowered the binoculars. "Riding a Nightmare."

"A witch?"

Valerius looked again. The rider wore black armor that seemed to drink the light. A serrated black sword was strapped to her back. Her hair was a jagged black mane.

But her face...

Valerius gasped. He had seen that face on posters. On the commemorative plates sold in the capital.

"It's her," he whispered. "The Chosen One."

"Lady Finriel?" the lieutenant asked, grabbing the binoculars. "She's... she looks different."

"Open the gate!" Valerius ordered. "Send an escort! She's returned!"

"Sir..." the lieutenant hesitated. "Look at the aura. The sensors are going crazy. That's not Light mana. That's Void."

"She's been in the wastes for months!" Valerius snapped. "Of course she picked up some taint! Open the gate! That is a direct order!"

The massive gates—inscribed with gold runes—began to groan open.

Finriel stopped her steed fifty yards from the entrance.

She looked up at the white walls. At the banners of the Alliance flapping in the artificial wind.

"Home," she whispered. The word tasted like poison.

"Lady Finriel!" Valerius shouted from the rampart. "Welcome back! We have medical teams on standby!"

Finriel looked at him. Her eyes were violet stars.

"Close the gate," she said. Her voice wasn't loud, but it carried perfectly.

"My Lady?" Valerius blinked.

"I said CLOSE IT!" Finriel roared.

Violet energy erupted from her. It hit the ground, sending a shockwave that rattled the teeth of every soldier on the wall.

"Sir... sensors detect massive hostile intent!" the sensor operator screamed. "Class 5 Event! Omega Level!"

"Class 5?" Valerius paled. "That's... that's a Demon Lord."

"Close the gate!" Valerius screamed. "ABORT! CLOSE IT!"

The gears ground in reverse. The heavy doors began to shut.

"Too late," Finriel whispered.

She drew *Grief*.

"Army," she said calmly. "Rise."

The shadows of the rocks around her lengthened. They stretched. They stood up.

Thousands of Shadow-Knights—Seccubus's gift, now hers—emerged from the ground.

"Kill them all," Finriel commanded. "But leave the Mana-Battery intact. I need it."

The Shadow Army surged forward.

## Part 2: The Slaughter

It wasn't a battle. It was an extermination.

The Alliance soldiers fought bravely. They fired mana-cannons. They launched arrows of light.

But the Shadow-Knights were relentless. They climbed the sheer walls like spiders. They phased through shields.

Finriel rode through the open gate.

A squad of Paladins charged her. "Traitor!" they screamed.

Finriel didn't even slow down. She swung *Grief*.

A wave of gravity crushed the Paladins into their own armor. They fell as crumpled tin cans.

She rode into the courtyard. It was chaos. Civilians were running. Screaming.

Finriel watched them. She remembered the thugs in the alley. The ungrateful woman with the bread.

*They are sheep,* she thought. *They need a shepherd.*

"Secure the perimeter!" she ordered.

A Shadow-Knight landed beside her. "The Mana-Battery is secure, My Queen."

"Good."

Finriel dismounted. She walked to the central tower, where the Mana-Battery—a massive crystal the size of a house—hummed with golden light. It was the anchor for this section of the Holy Dome.

She placed her hand on the crystal.

It burnt her. The Light rejected her.

"Shh," she soothed. "I'm not going to hurt you. I'm going to set you free."

She began to channel Void mana into the crystal.

It turned from gold to grey. Then to violet. Then to black.

The hum changed from a song to a scream.

"Corrupt," she commanded.

The crystal cracked.

## Part 3: The Message

The sky above Silverglade changed.

The golden dome that had protected them for centuries flickered. A massive crack—miles long—appeared in the zenith.

The sun—the real sun, the harsh, grey sun of the wasteland—shone through.

Finriel stood on the tower, watching the panic below.

"Valerius," she called out.

The Captain was on his knees, surrounded by Shadow-Knights. He was bleeding.

"Why?" Valerius wept. "You were our hope!"

"Hope is a lie," Finriel said. "I am Reality."

She knelt down, grabbing his chin.

"I am letting you live, Captain. Do you know why?"

"To mock me?"

"To carry a message," Finriel said. "Go to the Capital. Go to the High Council."

She leaned in close.

"Tell them the Chosen One is dead. Tell them the Grey Queen has risen. And tell them..."

She looked up at the cracked Dome.

"Tell them I am coming for the rest of the glass."

She stood up.

"Go."

Valerius scrambled up and ran. He ran towards the inner kingdoms.

Finriel watched him go.

"Burn the outpost," she ordered.

"My Queen!" A demon messenger landed. It was a Gargoyle. "Lord Malakor sends his regards. He asks for your next move."

Finriel looked at the burning fort. At the dead Paladins.

"My next move," she said, "is to wait."

"Wait? For what?"

Finriel touched her stomach. There was a weird sensation there. A hollow ache.

"For the other one," she whispered.

"The other one?"

"The Prophecy spoke of Twin Stars," Finriel turned to the demon. "I am the Falling Star. Somewhere... there must be a Rising Star."

She smiled. It was terrifying.

"And when he rises... I will be waiting."

## Part 4: The Legacy

**Deephollow**

Kaelen watched the sky darker. He felt the tremor in the earth when the Dome cracked.

"She did it," he whispered.

Lyra stood beside him. She was holding a blanket.

"She attacked Silverglade Gate," Lyra confirmed. "Zero survivors. Except one messenger."

"She's declared war on the world," Kaelen said.

He looked at his new weapon—a arm prosthesis made of dwarven steel, replacing the arm Finriel had shattered. (Wait, she shattered his sword, not his arm? Ah, I wrote he was 'broken', maybe I can retcon the injury to be severe).

"She needs to be stopped," Kaelen said.

"We can't stop her," Lyra said. "She's a Goddess now, Kaelen. She has the Demon King's backing."

"Then we need a God," Kaelen said.

He turned away from the wall.

"Find the Archives," he ordered. "Find everything we have on the Prophecy. Finriel was the 'Light'. But nothing exists without a shadow. If she is the Void... then the Light must have gone somewhere else."

"You think there's another?"

"There has to be," Kaelen said grimly. "Because if there isn't... we are all already dead."

## Part 5: The End of Innocence

**Deep in the Mines of the North**

Thousands of miles away, in a slave pit run by Dravos the Slaver, a boy was working.

He was ten years old. Skinny. Dirty. Covered in soot.

He hauled a cart of ore that was twice his weight.

"Move it, runt!" an overseer whipped him.

The boy didn't cry. He just gritted his teeth and pushed.

He had no name. They just called him "Boy."

He paused, wiping sweat from his forehead. He looked up at the crack in the ventilation shaft.

He saw the sky turn purple for a second. He felt... something.

A pull. A tug on his soul.

It felt like someone was calling him.

*Help me...*

It wasn't a voice. It was a feeling. A feeling of immense sadness.

The boy touched his chest. Under the dirt, on his skin, a faint golden mark began to glow.

It was small. Weak. But it was there.

"Boy! Get back to work!"

The overseer raised the whip again.

The boy looked at the overseer. His eyes—startlingly blue—flashed gold for a split second.

"I'm coming," the boy whispered to the voice in his head.

He picked up the cart. It felt lighter.

And in the darkness of the mine, a new star began to shine.
